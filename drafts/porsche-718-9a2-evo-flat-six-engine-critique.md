# Critique: Porsche 718 9A2 Evo Flat-Six Engine

## Critics Panel Score: 9.1 / 10 — PASS (threshold: 8.5)

### 1. Technical Accuracy — 9.0/10
Bore/stroke dimensions (102.0 × 81.5 mm), compression ratio (13.0:1), and power figures (394 hp GTS / 414 hp GT4 / 493 hp GT4 RS) match Porsche Newsroom and press data. Mean piston speed calculation at 7,800 rpm (21.2 m/s) is correct. The distinction between 9A2 Evo and 9A1 (GT3 engine) is accurately drawn. Preuninger quotes verified against Carscoops/Goodwood coverage. One minor nitpick: "99 horsepower per liter" is rounded from 98.6 — acceptable for prose.

### 2. Narrative Quality — 9.0/10
Opening hook ("has no business existing in 2026") earns attention without being clickbait. The Preuninger quote drops early and works as a structural fulcrum — after that quote, the reader trusts the article knows what it's talking about. Prose varies sentence length naturally. The closing section ("A Last Chapter Written on Purpose") elevates the piece from spec-sheet analysis to elegy without tipping into sentimentality. No AI slop. Zero em dashes.

### 3. Engineering Depth — 9.0/10
Piezo injector section is excellent — the solenoid-vs-crystal physics is explained without dumbing it down, and the five-pulse strategy connects directly to emissions compliance rather than just being stated. Cylinder deactivation section includes the alternating-bank strategy's thermal rationale (catalyst light-off temperature), which most coverage omits. Bore/stroke analysis correctly traces mechanical implications (mean piston speed, valve curtain area, rod angle geometry).

### 4. Source Quality — 8.5/10
Six sources spanning Porsche Newsroom (OEM primary), Autocar (technical journalism), Carscoops (first-party quotes), and DailyRevs (specifications). Good mix. Lacks SAE papers or Porsche Engineering Magazine references that could deepen the piezo injector claims, but for an enthusiast publication the sourcing is above average.

### 5. Reader Value / Insight — 9.5/10
The two-engine distinction (9A2 Evo vs 9A1) is the article's strongest contribution. Enthusiast forums routinely conflate the GTS and GT4 RS engines, and this piece dismantles that confusion with engineering specifics rather than trim-level marketing. The emissions-compliance framing — showing how cylinder deactivation's 11 g/km saving was the margin that made certification possible — gives readers a lens they won't find in standard reviews.

### 6. Structure / Pacing — 8.5/10
Clean section progression: overview → bore geometry → compression → fuel injection → cylinder deactivation → two-engine comparison → dry sump → firing order → conclusion. Each section has a clear thesis. The firing order section ("Boxer Sound") feels slightly tangential to the core narrative of "this engine shouldn't exist" but adds character and will resonate with enthusiasts.

### 7. Originality — 9.0/10
The "engineering defiance" angle is fresh. Most 718 coverage treats the 4.0-liter as a nice upgrade; this article frames it as an expensive act of institutional stubbornness against regulatory and economic gravity. The "102 millimeters" title concretizes the abstract concept into a physical dimension. The closing — "the only place to hear a 9A2 Evo at full song is behind the wheel of a car someone already bought" — is earned and original.
