# Critique: Ressence TYPE 10 DN — A Thermometer for Its Own Oil

**Article #240 | Category: Watches | Author: Marcus Thorne | Date: November 4, 2026**

---

## 1. Technical Accuracy — 89/100

Core specifications check out against multiple launch sources (Monochrome, Deployant, Fratello, Hourstriker, Haute Time, Notebookcheck): RW-04 with 252 components, twin barrels, 60-hour reserve, 28,800 vph; ROCS 10 with 196 components in oil; 2.95 ml silicone oil; magnetic transmission across a 0.1 mm titanium membrane with magnets 0.2 mm apart; 538 total components, 49 gears, 47 jewels; 41.5 mm Grade 5 titanium case with dark blue DLC; 82 grams; 5 ATM via Compression Lock System; caseback winding and setting; 100 pieces; CHF 45,000 excluding taxes; unveiled October 16, 2026 at WatchTime New York; shipping November 2026.

Minor deductions: the "nine micro-magnets" figure comes from a single source (Fratello) and is presented as launch-material fact; it should ideally carry a second confirmation. The green temperature range is stated as "roughly 25°C plus or minus 10°C" following Monochrome and Hourstriker, but Fratello's coverage phrases the range differently (10–25°C); the article's "roughly" hedging is appropriate but the discrepancy itself is not flagged to the reader. The claim that the runner "resists sudden changes to its rotation" as a flywheel damper is sound physics but is the article's inference from Ressence's "shock absorber" description, not a manufacturer-stated mechanism.

**Score: 89/100**

## 4. Materials Science — 87/100

Grade 5 titanium (Ti-6Al-4V class) is correctly positioned for the membrane role: thin, stiff, corrosion-resistant against silicone oil, and the density argument for the 82-gram total is sound. The HarDeCo DLC coating is identified. The refractive-index matching explanation for the oil display is accurate in principle: silicone oil's index is close to sapphire's, which suppresses internal reflections and the visible gaps between discs.

Missing: no discussion of the DLC coating's hardness or scratch behavior on a polished titanium case, which is the practical wear question for a dark-coated watch. The expansion membrane's material (likely a fatigue-resistant alloy given millions of thermal cycles) is not identified, and the article does not distinguish between the expansion membrane and a bellows, using the terms interchangeably. Silicone oil viscosity grade is not published by Ressence and is correctly not invented.

**Score: 87/100**

## 5. Horological Depth — 90/100

The serial-coupled twin-barrel torque-flattening explanation is correct and correctly tied to the viscous load, which is the article's strongest technical thread. The magnetic-coupling efficiency gap is flagged as an unpublished number rather than papered over, which is exactly the scholarly honesty the site requires. The crownless caseback-setting trade-off is traced to seal architecture rather than presented as a quirk. The runner-as-flywheel-damper reading connects the 120-second indication to coupling stability in a way launch coverage does not.

Minor deduction: the article does not address whether the magnetic coupling introduces any rate variation under shock (momentary phase slip would show as a timekeeping error, not just a display glitch), which is the natural follow-up question for a technically literate reader.

**Score: 90/100**
