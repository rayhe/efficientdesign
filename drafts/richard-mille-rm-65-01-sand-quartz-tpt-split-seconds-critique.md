# Critique: Six Columns, Two Hands, One Tenth of a Second — Richard Mille RM 65-01 Sand Quartz TPT
**Article #210 | Marcus Thorne | October 5, 2026**

## 1. Technical Accuracy (8.5/10)

Specs cross-verified across multiple independent sources. RMAC4 dimensions (31.78 x 29.98 mm, 8.69 mm thick, 51 jewels, 600+ parts) agree between Hodinkee's 2023 spec table, the K2 Luxury listing, and YourWatchHub. Frequency (5 Hz / 36,000 vph), 60h reserve (plus or minus 10%), case dims (44.50 x 49.94 mm, 16.10 mm thick, 50m WR) consistent everywhere. The six-column-wheel count, the article's headline fact, is corroborated by Revolution Watch and Tatler Asia in addition to the dealer listing, which is the right sourcing discipline for a claim Richard Mille's own materials do not document.

Minor issues: (a) the resin-chemistry section generalizes composite curing behavior to argue why colorways arrive years apart. The reasoning is sound and explicitly framed as reasoning, but no source confirms RM's color qualification process specifically. Fine as stated. (b) The part-count figure (600+) applies to the base RMAC4; the McLaren W1 variant is reported at 480 components. The article correctly uses the base figure. (c) El Primero 1969 reserve cited as "closer to 50 hours" — the standard figure for the 3019 PHC is 50h, correct.

## 2. Voice & Style (9.0/10)

Distinct Marcus Thorne voice. The roll cage case analogy lands immediately and is never belabored. "Treat the colorway as the invitation and the RMAC4 calibre as the reason to accept" is the kind of opening line that earns the reader. Opinions are present and calibrated: "new paint on an old brief. But the brief was sound, and the paint required real chemistry."

The closing anaphora ("the correct instrument / the correct control architecture / the correct answer") is a deliberate rhetorical device that works because each instance is argued, not asserted. Sentence rhythm has good variance — terse fragments ("The chemistry says otherwise.") alternate with long explanatory builds.

## 6. Automotive Crossover (9.5/10)

This is the article's strongest dimension and the site's editorial identity. Roll cage case, engine-mount shock absorbers (the ISO SW rubbers), variable-geometry turbocharger parallel for the rotor, gear-position display for the function selector, pit-wall lap timing as the honest use case. ## 7. Reader Value (9.0/10)

An Efficient Design reader learns real engineering: why stopping a hand while its train runs is the hard part of a rattrapante (clutch friction, amplitude loss), why 5 Hz is a resolution requirement not a marketing number, why column wheels beat cams for crisp sequencing, how resin-cure chemistry constrains colorway cadence, and why vertical coupling matters for measurement.
