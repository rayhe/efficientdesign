## Critic 1: Technical Accuracy (Dr. Miriam Ostfeld, Materials Science)
**Score: 9.0/10**

The materials claims check out. Magnesium at 1.74 g/cm³ is correctly stated, and the density comparisons (two-thirds of aluminum, one-quarter of steel) are right. The four-segment magnesium architecture (front roof frame, two panel bows, rear window frame) comes straight from Porsche's own newsroom release, as does the single-hydraulic-cylinder-per-side kinematic coupling and the 23 × 55 cm stowed package. The weight figures (187 lbs of roof hardware, +198 vs Carrera 4 coupe, +44 vs Cabriolet, 10 mm CoG rise) are all from Autoblog's first drive and are presented with the correct deltas.

The die-casting claim for pivot hardware is presented as engineering reasoning rather than a Porsche-published fact, and the article's disclosure covers mechanism analysis as informed architecture. Fair. One caution: the newsroom source describes the panel-bow top in the context of the 992 Cabriolet's 2019 launch, and the article applies the architecture to the Targa roof, which shares the panel-bow system but adds the glasshouse choreography. The article is transparent about this lineage. The AWD-only statement for the 992 Targa is correct for the generation (all variants carry the "4" designation).

Deduction: 0.5 for the die-cast pivot claim being reasoned rather than sourced, 0.5 for not explicitly noting that the 2019 newsroom release describes the Cabriolet program that the Targa mechanism builds on (the article implies it but could state it once, plainly).

## Critic 2: Engineering Depth (Prof. Akira Taniguchi, Micromechanical Systems)
**Score: 9.5/10**

The strongest engineering article in the recent cars run. The central insight, that the Targa roof is an under-actuated mechanism where the sequencing lives in the linkage geometry rather than in a controller, is the correct frame and it is original to this piece. Most coverage of the Targa roof stops at "watch the cool video." This article asks the better question: why does one cylinder per side suffice, and what does that imply about the linkage synthesis problem someone in Weissach had to solve.

The weight ledger section is honest engineering accounting: 187 pounds is stated as a cost, not hand-waved, and the 10 mm CoG rise plus rearward shift are connected to the AWD-only configuration as a coherent chassis decision. The observation that the parking sensors are repurposed as mechanism protection is the kind of cross-system thinking the site exists to celebrate. The electric-central-closure / hydraulic-folding duty split is correctly characterized.

Deduction: 0.5 for not addressing hydraulic system pressure or fluid type, which would have completed the actuation picture. Acknowledged as unpublished by Porsche and covered by the disclosure, but the gap is real.

## Critic 4: Originality & Contribution (James Holbrook, Automotive Journalism)
**Score: 9.0/10**

This is not a press-release summary. Existing Targa roof coverage falls into two camps: launch-day "watch it move" videos and spec-list reviews. Nobody has written the kinematic-coupling story: that two actuators drive four segments through machined linkage logic, and that this represents a deliberate philosophical choice against the industry's add-another-motor instinct. The magnesium pivot-tolerance argument (tenths of a millimeter of slop multiplied across four segments against a 23 cm stowage envelope) is original analysis built from published dimensions.

The watchmaking parallel (column wheel as mechanical control logic) is a genuine crossover contribution that fits the site's dual mandate without forcing the comparison.

Deduction: 1.0 for not addressing the 991-to-992 evolution in mechanism detail. The article notes the choreography is "refined rather than replaced" but does not say what changed between generations, which a reader of an engineering site will wonder about.
