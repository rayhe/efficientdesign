### 1. Technical Accuracy — 8.5/10
- Bi-Compound Technology description matches Michelin's official product documentation (high molecular chain elastomer outer, rigid elastomer inner)
- 10% thicker outer compound zone on Cup 2 R vs Cup 2 verified through RPM Technik and Michelin specs
- 0.5 s/km advantage claim matches RPM Technik's published testing notes and Michelin product documentation
- Nürburgring 6:49.328 lap time confirmed by Tire Technology International, Carscoops, and Porsche press materials; driver was Jörg Bergmeister (article says Lars Kern and Kevin Estre — Kern set the GT3 RS MR record, Estre set GT2 RS records; Bergmeister drove the standard GT3 RS 6:49.328). Minor conflation: article conflates drivers across different GT3 RS variants. Not incorrect that Kern and Estre use the specs, but the 6:49.328 specifically was Bergmeister.
- 275/35ZR20 front, 335/30ZR21 rear tire sizes match published Porsche specifications
- Billy Johnson / MotoIQ bespoke tire development process verified (GT350/R, GT500, Ford GT programs)
- Continental 1992/1994 silica introduction dates: Michelin first in 1992, Continental followed in 1994 with documented ~50% wet braking improvement — accurate
- EU label ratings (F-G rolling resistance, C-D wet grip for Cup 2 R vs E-F and B-C for standard Cup 2) would benefit from size-specific verification — ratings vary by dimension
- UTQG 180 AA A consistent with Michelin specs via Quattro Tires
- Variable Contact Patch 3.0 and Dynamic Response Technology accurately described
- Tyre Reviews 4-tire test: lineup correctly identified (Trofeo RS, Cup 2 R, Bridgestone Potenza Race, Vitour); test conducted in 2025 at European proving ground; Pirelli overall winner confirmed by multiple sources. Original draft had incorrect percentage scores (98.2%/94.6% were from a different 3-tire test) — corrected during review to qualitative results.

- Jerome Mondain identified as Michelin WEC technical director — confirmed via Sportscar365 Q&A

### 2. Narrative Flow — 9.0/10
- Opening paragraph is exceptionally strong: the "four postcards" metaphor creates an immediate, visceral image that carries through the entire piece
- Logical progression: contact patch framing → bi-compound lateral → skim coat vertical → Le Mans heritage → bespoke OEM programs → filler chemistry → sidewall engineering → limitations → closing
- Each section transitions cleanly: "Bi-compound is the lateral story. Skim coating is the vertical one." — efficient and direct
- "Where It Falls Short" section demonstrates editorial integrity and breaks the otherwise promotional trajectory
- The closing paragraph's honest disclaimer ("Full disclosure: I have not driven on Cup 2 R tires") is refreshingly candid and builds credibility
- One structural critique: the silica/carbon black section sits slightly late in the narrative — a reader already 2,000 words deep might have preferred this context earlier, before the OEM bespoke section, since filler composition explains why different tunes are possible

### 3. Engineering Depth — 9.0/10
- Bi-compound technology explained at the molecular level: hysteresis in polymer chains, mechanical adhesion, slip angle behavior — not just marketing description
- Skim coat technology section is a genuine knowledge contribution: most tire coverage never mentions sub-surface layering or its effect on grip progression through heat cycles
- Le Mans technology transfer described bidirectionally (race → road and road → race) with named source (Jerome Mondain)
- OEM co-development process detailed through Billy Johnson's firsthand account: three independent variables (tread, compound, carcass), nonlinear optimization, vehicle-specific tuning
- Silica vs carbon black filler section goes to atomic scale (neutron spin-echo spectroscopy, polybutadiene-carbon black interaction) — impressive depth
- Dynamic Response Technology: aramid fibers, contact patch geometry under load, comparison to Kevlar's tensile properties
- Missing: vulcanization chemistry could have been explored further — the article mentions "vulcanization profile" but doesn't explain cross-link density or sulfur bridge formation, which directly determines compound character
- Missing: no mention of tire construction methods (drum-built vs flat-tread) or the specific manufacturing challenge of placing two compounds side-by-side during the building process
