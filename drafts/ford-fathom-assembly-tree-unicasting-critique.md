# Critique — ford-fathom-assembly-tree-unicasting (#207)

Panel: 7 critics, threshold 8.5+. Round 1, 2026-10-02.

## 1. Materials Scientist — 9/10
LFP characterization is accurate: cobalt-free, nickel-free, cheaper raw materials, longer cycle life, and a meaningfully more benign thermal-runaway profile than nickel chemistries. The cold-weather weakness (slow charging, reduced usable capacity) is stated honestly and tied to the Michigan-plant bet, which is the correct engineering tension to highlight. Aluminum unicasting described without inventing an alloy designation. The Bühler 9,000-ton figure is carried with its provenance (trade newsletter via RepairerDrivenNews) and hedged in-text as "reported." No material claim overreaches its source.

## 2. Manufacturing Engineer — 9/10
The assembly-tree analysis is mechanically sound: serial takt coupling is the real constraint of a moving line, and building the battery-plus-interior module at ergonomic height on its own takt is the genuine ergonomic and logistical win. "Structurally complete" castings enabling pre-join population of modules is the correct causal link between unicasting and the tree layout; the piece states it explicitly rather than implying it. The zonal-architecture inference from the 1.3 km harness reduction is properly fenced ("Ford has not said the words... I will not put them in the company's mouth"). One romanticism: "rigidity arrives at the casting machine" compresses heat-treat, trim, and machining steps, but the module-logic point stands. No errors found.

## 6. Skeptic — 9/10

The $19.5B write-down context is given its own paragraph rather than buried. Two genuine counterweights added beyond the press kit: repairability/total-loss risk of single-piece castings (with the Model Y insurance observable) and tooling concentration risk (one die, one plant). The zonal inference is fenced. Price appears only as the output of the engineering arithmetic, not as pricing coverage.
