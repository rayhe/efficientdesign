## 1. Technical Accuracy (8.9/10)

Core news claims are sourced: GM service bulletin N262573420 dated October 8, 2026; 11 units of the 2026 CT5-V Blackwing with Precision Package; remedy part 85829248 (two assemblies) plus four bolts; free through October 31, 2028; dealers do not install per the bulletin (gmauthority, Oct 26). Precision Package contents match Cadillac's own package-modal copy: stiffer springs, larger front stabilizer bar, revised bushings, redesigned front knuckles for aggressive camber, increased-rate rear knuckle bushings, revised front ride-link/rear lateral-link bushings, unique billet aluminum rear toe links, recalibrated Magnetic Ride Control, revised steering/chassis calibrations, eLSD adjustments, carbon ceramic brakes. $18,000 option, 2025 MY introduction, per gmauthority.

LT4 facts hold: 668 hp / 659 lb-ft, single-builder assembly at Bowling Green Performance Build Center, signed plaque on the supercharger lid (topspeed, 2021, via Blackwing chief engineer Mirza Grebovic). Eaton TVS R1740 1.7L and direct-injection-only are per autoevolution. Tremec TR-6060 with no-lift shift and active rev matching, ~50% manual take rate in 2023, per carbuzz. The $6,275 manual-vs-auto factory-price gap matches hotcars' breakdown ($3,675 transmission + $2,600 Super Cruise). August 2026 inventory count of 64 manuals nationwide and year-end 2026 production end per hotcars. Roma quotes, next-gen RWD platform confirmation, and NA V8 + manual for the CT5-V are per thedrive (Oct 15). McQuaid carve-out per stickshifting (Oct 23). The Drive 2026 COTY + Best Performance Car per thedrive.

Compliance steer explanation is standard suspension engineering, correctly hedged in the honesty block as applied rather than measured. The claim that the Precision alignment window "cannot exist" without the billet link is the article's interpretive load-bearing assertion; it is argued from Cadillac's own "unique to the package" framing plus the bulletin evidence, and presented as inference, which is fair.

One fix applied: 0-60 variance (3.4 vs 3.7 s across sources) added to the Unresolved row.

## 2. Engineering Depth (8.8/10)

The article earns its chassis-engineering billing. Compliance steer is explained from first principles (knuckle twist, bushing deflection, geometry snap-back) rather than jargon-dropped. The billet-vs-steel distinction is correctly split into tolerance/repeatability and stiffness, with the honest note that weight is the secondary advantage. The load-bearing sentence "the alignment the package sells cannot exist without them" is the article's thesis and it is defended, not asserted. The eLSD/knuckle/bushing list is treated as a system (geometry, damping, calibration) rather than a parts dump. The 10L90 torque-converter reference in the no-lift-shift aside is correct (the 10-speed auto is a converter box), and the no-lift boost retention point is real.

Could go one step deeper on toe-link load paths (which loads dominate: braking vs cornering entry) but the sources don't instrument it and inventing numbers would be worse than the qualitative treatment.
