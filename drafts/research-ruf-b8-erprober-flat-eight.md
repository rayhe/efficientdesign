# Research: RUF B8 "Erprober" 4.8L twin-turbo flat-eight
Article #191 (cars) — Elena Voss

## Sourced facts
- RUF B8 "Erprober" (German for "tester"): 4.8-liter twin-turbocharged flat-eight, >1,000 hp, >1,000 Nm (737-738 lb-ft). Debuted Goodwood Festival of Speed, July 2026.
- First boxer-eight in RUF history; designed and developed in-house. RUF says neither it nor any other company has deployed such an engine "in this form."
- Test mule: modified CTR3, body stretched 100 mm (3.9 in) to fit the longer block. Six-speed manual, RUF-developed, driving rear wheels. Livery in Blossom Yellow referencing the CTR Yellowbird.
- Hillclimb runs twice daily Jul 10-12, 2026, driven by Tanner Foust; displayed in Supercar Paddock between runs. Lubricant partner: MOTUL.
- Not for immediate production; dedicated testbed for technologies shaping a future RUF model. RUF has been a standalone manufacturer (own VINs) since the early 1980s, not a tuner.
- CarBuzz/others: no series-production car has ever had a flat-eight. Last flat-eight in a road car: Porsche 914/8 prototypes (two built), flat-eight from the 908 race car, ~50 years ago.
- Autocar: Porsche once created a concept for a 918 Spyder successor called the 904 with a 5.0-liter flat-eight at ~750 bhp and 738 lb-ft. Never produced.

## First-principles / original analysis (label as such in article)
- Specific output: 1000 hp / 4.8 L = ~208 hp/L. A 3.6L flat-six to 1000 hp would need ~278 hp/L. More swept volume = lower peak cylinder pressure per cylinder at a given power target, wider knock margin, less aggressive ignition timing, lower thermal load per piston/crown. This is the "displacement as structural answer" thesis.
- Flat-eight balance: four mirror-image piston pairs cancel primary reciprocating forces by construction. Porsche's 908 proved the architecture is smooth enough to run at 8,400+ rpm. Phasing two flat-four crank sections lets much of the secondary shake cancel as well.
- Firing interval: 720 deg / 8 cylinders = 90 deg crank between power pulses. Extremely smooth delivery; turbos see near-continuous exhaust energy.
- Packaging: flat-eight adds roughly one cylinder-pair of length vs a flat-six at the same bore spacing (approx +1/3 block length). Width unchanged; CG stays low. The 100 mm CTR3 stretch is the visible evidence. In a rear-engine layout, length is the scarce dimension, not width.
- The risk nobody talks about: long crankshaft torsional vibration. An eight-cylinder flat crank is long; harmonics and damper design are the real engineering bill. This is likely the quiet reason Porsche never productionized a flat-eight road engine.
- Six-speed manual at 1,000 Nm: gearbox torque capacity is the binding constraint; manuals at four-figure torque are rare (BMW/Ferrari have abandoned them). RUF builds its own gearboxes; the manual is the statement of intent.

## Limitations
- RUF disclosed no bore, stroke, compression ratio, redline, turbo sizing, or engine weight. Do not invent.
- No hands-on: have not heard the engine, seen it on a stand, or reviewed RUF's internal data.
- Porsche 908 spec from general knowledge: 1968-70, 3.0L air-cooled SOHC flat-eight, ~350 hp. Well-established but not re-verified from a primary source in this run; frame carefully.
