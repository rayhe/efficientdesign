# Critique: 388 Parts to Tell Time in Digits
## A. Lange & Söhne Zeitwerk Mechanical Digital Display

### Critic 1: Technical Accuracy — 9.0/10
The article accurately describes the remontoire d'égalité mechanism, the three-disc architecture, and the fly governor's function. The description of the pin-and-slot coupling that eliminated the phantom tick aligns with Hodinkee and Monochrome Watches reporting. The 388-component count, 18,000 bph frequency, and 41.9mm case diameter check out against Lange's published specs. Minor deduction: the article states the stop-work halts at "36 hours" but doesn't specify whether Gen 2's stop-work triggers at a different point in the 72-hour reserve. The Pallweber and Semper Opera House historical context is well-researched and accurate.

### Critic 2: Narrative & Voice — 9.0/10
Strong opening that subverts expectations ("Watch hands lie to you"). The George Daniels quotation is perfectly placed — dryly relevant and provides authority. "Building it nearly killed the project, and not just once" is an effective short-sentence hook. The article maintains the engineering-appreciation tone throughout without drifting into price-list journalism. The "phantom tick" section reads like narrative nonfiction. Slight repetition of the word "crisp" in describing disc transitions. Good closing that circles back to the opening premise.

### Critic 3: Source Quality — 8.5/10
Ten cited sources including Lange's own technical documentation, SJX Watches' comparative analysis, Hodinkee technical perspectives, Monochrome Watches collection guides, George Daniels' Watchmaking, and Fratello Watches hands-on review. The SJX comparison of Zeitwerk vs Vagabondage III vs Opus 3 is the strongest analytical source. Limitation disclosure is present ("I have not handled the watch" equivalent is implicit). Deduction: some claims about first-year teething problems lack a specific named source. The Robert Hoffmann attribution for the 70-80% redesign claim should ideally cite which publication reported it.

### Critic 4: Structural Engineering — 9.0/10
Logical progression from problem statement (why digital is hard) → solution (remontoire) → implementation (three discs) → refinement (phantom tick fix) → usability (setting/power reserve) → context (history/competition) → honest assessment (limitations). Each section builds on the previous one. The FAQ schema in the structured data is well-formed. The pull-stat (388 parts comparison) provides an immediate visual anchor. No orphaned sections or dead-end tangents.
