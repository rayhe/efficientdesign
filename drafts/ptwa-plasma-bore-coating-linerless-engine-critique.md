# Critique: PTWA Plasma Bore Coating — Linerless Engine
**Article #159 — efficientdesign.net**
**Date:** 2026-08-15

## 7-Critic Panel

### 1. Technical Accuracy — 9/10
Every major engineering claim is sourced and correct. Ford PTWA details (400 rpm, 0.0625-in wire, 252 ft/min, 0.004-in finished thickness) match WardsAuto and MotorTrend primary sources. The Voodoo bore spacing claim (6mm vs 7.8mm) is attributed to Palazzolo. BMW LDS thickness (0.3mm) matches BMW B58 training documents. The critical correction — that GM's LT6/LT7 use cast-in iron liners, NOT thermal spray — is backed by CorvSport technical specs showing "A319 cast aluminum with cast-in iron cylinder liners." Nikasil sulfur failure history is well-documented. Minor deduction: the "half-millimeter error in groove depth changes adhesion strength by double-digit percentages" claim in the surface prep paragraph is reasonable but not sourced to a specific study. The M139 "416 hp from two liters" is correct (the A45 S makes 416 hp).

### 3. Materials Science — 9/10
Iron-based coating chemistry vs. Nikasil nickel matrix is correctly distinguished. The microstructure description (molten droplets flattening, solidifying in microseconds, building layer on layer) accurately describes thermal spray splat formation. The mechanical interlocking surface prep (dovetail undercuts) is correctly characterized. The distinction between PTWA (wire + plasma arc), LDS (wire + electric arc), NANOSLIDE (twin-wire arc), and SUMEbore (powder + atmospheric plasma) shows genuine understanding of the thermal spray family. The interference-fit thermal boundary explanation is physically correct — air trapped at microscopic surface irregularities does act as insulation. One minor note: calling the S58 a "triple-turbo" would be incorrect (it's twin-turbo). Checking the text — it does NOT say triple-turbo, it just lists the S58 by name. Clean.

### 4. Readability — 8/10
Accessible to enthusiasts who know what a piston ring is, without dumbing down the metallurgy or thermodynamics. The analogies work well (dovetail joint, cheese grater, watchmaker nodding). The structure flows logically: what PTWA is → how it works → what it improves → who else uses it → what went wrong before → what you lose → opinion. Deduction: some sentences run very long (40-60+ words) which may lose casual readers, especially in the BMW and GM comparison paragraphs. A few strategic paragraph breaks within those sections would help. But this is an engineering blog for enthusiasts, not a newspaper, so the audience tolerance is higher.

Benefits section ranks advantages by engineering interest rather than by marketing order. "Not Just Ford" section efficiently surveys the competitive landscape. The Nikasil cautionary tale is placed perfectly — right after showing the technology works, you learn what happens when the chemistry fails. The "What You Give Up" section is honest about trade-offs, and the GM comparison is the article's sharpest analytical insight. Closing opinion section earns its right to exist.
