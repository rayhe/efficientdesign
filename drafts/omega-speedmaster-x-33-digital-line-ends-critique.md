Every load-bearing number was verified against a primary or reputable secondary source before drafting, and re-checked in the fact-check pass below.

- Prices: Skywalker $5,900 (Hodinkee); Marstimer CHF 5,900 ex VAT / ~$7,000 US (Fratello, Exquisite Timepieces). Correct.
- One correction caught in review: the draft originally claimed the X-33 was "roughly a hundred times more accurate" than a typical quartz watch. Arithmetic says 0.07 s/day is ~2.1 s/month against ~15 s/month typical quartz, about 7x, and ~70x against a COSC mechanical allowance. Fixed before shipping.
- Minor softness: "400 kilometers below you" for the nearest watchmaker is an ISS-orbit approximation, phrased colloquially and clearly approximate. Acceptable.

### 2. Engineering Depth — 9.0
The thermo-compensation explanation is the article's spine and it earns its place: quartz drift with temperature, sensor plus correction table, TCXO as embedded metrology with no moving parts. The Mars-time section treats MTC, leap sols, and the solar compass as a second calendar system rather than a gimmick, and the 80 dB alarm caseback-aperture detail plus the two-year battery and power-saving mode show mission-first thinking throughout. The table of four generations compresses 28 years into one scannable object. What keeps this from 9.5: no treatment of how the ana-digi display is actually driven (shared stepper vs. independent LCD driver) and no discussion of the Z-33 sibling, which would have rounded out the family tree. Both are honest gaps, not errors.

### 5. Originality — 8.8
Not a press-release summary. Three original contributions: (a) the qualification-inertia thesis, that NASA flies the 2001 watch because requalification costs more than obsolescence, and the same logic that kept the Moonwatch alive now keeps the Gen 2 alive; (b) the catalog-removal vs. discontinuation precision, sourced to Watchlopedia's careful reading, which most coverage elided; (c) the Mars-time-as-second-calendar framing, including the leap-sol arithmetic as a compounding 39-minute daily error. The "eight years combined public sale" synthesis (Gen 1 3y + Gen 2 5y) is a small original computation. The piece adds to the conversation rather than restating Fratello.
