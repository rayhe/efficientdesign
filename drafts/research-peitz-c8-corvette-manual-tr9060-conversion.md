# Research: Peitz Performance C8 Corvette Manual Conversion (Tremec TR-9060)

**Article #241 | Category: Cars | Author: Marcus Thorne | Date: November 5, 2026**

## News hook
- Hot Rod, November 3, 2026: "C8 Corvette TREMEC Manual Transmission Conversion" — Peitz Performance (Texas) put Tremec's six-speed manual transaxle into a C8 Corvette.
- GM Authority, November 4, 2026: "Peitz Performance Releases C8 Corvette Manual Conversion" — first video of the car in motion.
- Corvette chief engineer Tony Roma has said GM has no plans to offer a manual C8.

## The gearbox: Tremec TR-9060
- Six-speed manual transaxle, debuted publicly at SEMA 2025.
- Designed to share the TR-9080 DCT's packaging envelope and mounting points (the factory C8 dual-clutch transaxle). Same case profile, same suspension hard points.
- Rated 1,000 hp, 800 lb-ft torque, maximum input 8,600 rpm.
- Six forward gears plus reverse, helical-cut for strength with lower noise; mechanical limited-slip differential; cable-shift mechanism; aluminum construction; no park lock.
- Built on Tremec's experience developing the TR-9080 DCT for the C8.

## The conversion (Peitz Performance, owner Alex Peitz)
- Development car is a track-prepared C8 the shop campaigns in Gridlife and other series; the entire car was 3D-scanned during earlier race builds, which let the team plan shifter, linkage, clutch pedal, and supporting parts from the computer model before cutting anything.
- Shifter: Porsche aftermarket shifter as the starting point, rebuilt into a custom six-speed mechanism; cable length estimated from the 3D model, refined over a few trial fitments.
- Clutch pedal: third pedal taken from a Cadillac CT4-V Blackwing, modified with a pedal stop and hydraulic clutch assist; bracket mounts it to the brake pedal assembly under the dash.
- Flywheel/clutch: McLeod built a flywheel incorporating the ring gear and teeth from a C8 flywheel, plus the clutch.
- Electronics: factory electronics replaced with a MoTeC plug-and-play setup designed by Peitz for the C8. M182 controls the engine; secondary M150 handles transmission functions, either factory DCT control or ignition cuts for upshifts/downshifts with the manual box. Uses the factory C8 wiring harness with custom connectors. Production unit intended to work with factory dash, ABS, power steering, and other stock systems.
- Standalone DCT controller also offered: M150 alone to run Tremec TR-9080 and TR-9070 dual-clutch transmissions, for kit cars and restomods.
- Goal: sell the conversion as a complete package under one part number. MoTeC system available for purchase around publication time; estimated ship dates Q1 2027.
- Hot Rod first-drive account: short throws, precise shifter feel; DCT car would likely remain quicker in outright acceleration.

## Original angle
The gearbox is the least interesting part of this story, and that is the point. Tremec did the hard packaging work a year ago at SEMA 2025, building a manual transaxle that bolts to the same points as the DCT. What Peitz solved is everything around the gearbox: the pedal geometry under a dash that was never drawn with a third pedal, the cable routing through a mid-engine tunnel, and the electronics handshake with a car whose factory brain assumes a dual-clutch is always attached. The conversion is a study in integration, not invention. Also the parts-bin irony: a Porsche shifter and a Cadillac pedal, assembled by a Texas race shop, in the car GM insists cannot have a manual. The honest trade is stated plainly: the manual will be slower than the DCT and nobody involved pretends otherwise.

## Limitations / open questions
- No pricing published for the conversion package yet.
- No independent performance numbers (0-60, lap times) for the converted car; development car is track-prepared, so impressions may not transfer to a stock C8.
- Long-term durability of the ignition-cut shift strategy under track abuse is unproven in customer hands.
- No park lock on the TR-9060: parking procedure for a street car (gear + handbrake) is a real usability note, not yet addressed in coverage.
- I have not driven the car; analysis rests on Hot Rod and GM Authority coverage plus Tremec's published TR-9060 specifications.
