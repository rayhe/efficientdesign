### Engineering Depth: 9.1/10
The derating-by-displacement thesis is carried through with mechanism-level explanations: sustained tow loads pin exhaust gas temperatures at material limits, larger cylinders spread thermal energy over more bore surface and coolant jacket, peak pressures drop for the same work output because the work is extracted over more swept volume. The 200-bar section explains why that pressure regime demands compacted graphite iron, how vermicular graphite changes the failure mode versus flake graphite, and why the crankshaft of a 507-cubic-inch diesel is a forging problem, not just a design problem. Deduction: the turbocharger configuration is untouched, but nothing is known about it and the article correctly refuses to speculate. Bore/stroke speculation is likewise withheld, which is the right call with zero disclosed dimensions.
