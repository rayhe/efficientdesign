# Research: Girard-Perregaux Neo Constant Escapement — Silicon Bi-Stable Blade

## The Fundamental Problem
- Mainspring torque decreases as it unwinds — high when fully wound, weak near depletion
- Variable torque → variable impulse to balance wheel → variable amplitude → variable rate
- Even good end curves on balance springs only partially compensate
- Historical solutions: fusée-and-chain (upstream torque equalization), remontoir d'égalité (secondary spring), Maltese cross (limiting power range). All add complexity, cost, fragility, or reduce power reserve
- Key distinction: fusée/remontoir act UPSTREAM of escapement. A constant-force escapement acts WITHIN the escapement itself

## Origin Story
- Late 1990s: Nicolas Déhon (then at Rolex) playing with a train ticket on his commute
- Pressed card curved into C-shape. Pushed against the arc → card snapped to opposite curvature
- Key insight: whether pressed slowly or forcefully, at the buckling point it ALWAYS snapped back with the same force
- Bistable blade = a mechanism that stores and releases the same energy regardless of input force
- Patent filed at Rolex, but metallic materials couldn't produce a reliable blade. Project shelved
- Déhon moved to Girard-Perregaux. Silicon revolution enabled the concept

## Development Timeline
- 2008: First concept watch with silicon spring unveiled by GP
- 2013: Constant Escapement L.M. — first production watch (small series). 48mm white gold, off-center display. Won Aiguille d'Or at GPHG (Grand Prix d'Horlogerie de Genève)
- 2023: Neo Constant Escapement — revised geometry, 45mm, 266 components (reduced by 16 from original), centered hands, self-starting, COSC-certified. 13+ new patents (15 total across both generations)
- 2026: New editions — 18K rose gold (regular collection) + carbon-silicon composite (limited to 2 pieces, 45.35mm, green silicon blade variant)

## How It Works (Detailed)
- Movement has a "fifth wheel" on the gear train (unusual)
- Fifth wheel drives TWO escape wheels (3 lobed teeth each), positioned on either side
- Both wheels rotate in same direction, driven directly by fifth wheel
- A butterfly-shaped silicon frame spans between them
- At center: an ultra-thin silicon blade spring (14 microns wide, 120 microns thick)
- A pivot lever divides the blade at center and engages the balance roller
- Cycle: Balance swings → lever twists blade into S-shape → blade reaches critical buckling point → SNAPS to opposite S-curve
- The snap ALWAYS releases the same stored elastic energy regardless of input torque
- This delivers constant, fixed impulse to balance wheel

### Neo Revision (2023)
- Arming rocker now locked/unlocked by smaller co-axial escape wheels
- Larger 3-lobed wheels no longer perform escapement work — they serve as rotational mass
- Larger wheels temper sudden acceleration of smaller wheels at each release
- Absorb shocks, stabilize arming rocker motion
- Result: improved efficiency, better self-starting behavior

## Silicon Blade Manufacturing (at Sigatec, Sion, Switzerland)
- Cleanroom laboratory conditions
- Start: large silicon crystal → sawn into wafer-thin slices (wafers)
- Silicon wafer bonded to silicon substrate via oxide layer (depth reference for etching)
- Liquid polymer applied while wafer spins at high speed → centrifugal force creates smooth, thin photoresist layer
- Photolithography: mask positioned like a stencil, UV light triggers chemical reaction
- Solution removes photoresist traces, revealing balance spring outlines
- DRIE (Deep Reactive Ion Etching): silicon removed layer by layer (inverse of 3D printing) until oxide layer reached
- Stripping: substrate and oxide layer removed
- Thermal treatment: creates new oxide layer → increases mechanical strength → produces characteristic blue-to-violet color
- Hand-detachment: each spring carefully removed from wafer by hand
- Yield: only 30 escapement springs per wafer (vs 500 conventional hairsprings per wafer = ~17x lower yield)
- Final dimensions: 120μm thick, 14μm wide blade (human hair = 50-90μm, so blade is 3.5-6.4x thinner)

## Technical Specifications — Neo Constant Escapement
- Caliber: GP09200 (variants: GP09200-1153 Ti, GP09200-2680 rose gold)
- Type: hand-wound, COSC-certified
- Diameter: 39.50mm (17½ lignes), thickness: 7.40mm
- Components: 266 parts, 29 jewels
- Frequency: 21,600 vph (3 Hz)
- Power reserve: minimum 7 days (168 hours) via double barrel
- Functions: hours, minutes, central seconds, linear power reserve indicator
- Patents: 15

## Production Constraints
- Only 5-6 watchmakers qualified to assemble
- Expected output: 50-60 watches per year
- Each watchmaker assembles from start to finish

## Historical Context
- GP has a constant-pursuit legacy:
  - 1867: Constant Girard's three-bridge tourbillon (parallel bridges design)
  - 1966: Century Prize from Neuchâtel Observatory for 36,000 vph high-frequency movement
  - 1970: First industrially manufactured quartz watch (32,768 Hz — standard still used today)
- Sigatec: co-founded by Ulysse Nardin in 2006, largest silicon component manufacturer for watches
- Silicon in watchmaking pioneered by UN (Freak 2001), now industry standard

## Comparisons to Other Constant-Force Solutions
- Breguet Magnetic Escapement (covered in our article): uses magnetic potential energy. Magnets on a rocker interact with fixed magnets to create constant-force impulse. Different physics (magnetic vs elastic)
- FP Journe Remontoir d'Égalité: secondary spring rewound periodically. Acts upstream, not within escapement
- A. Lange & Söhne PLM fusée-and-chain: torque equalization at barrel level
- GP is unique: constant force built INTO the escapement mechanism itself via bistable silicon blade
