# Research: Gordon Murray T.50 — Fan-Assisted Aerodynamics and Cosworth V12

## Topic
The Gordon Murray T.50's fan-assisted ground-effect aerodynamics and Cosworth GMA V12: how a concept banned from Formula 1 in 1978 returned as the most advanced aerodynamic system on any road car, paired with the highest-revving, lightest V12 engine ever built for street use.

## Key Engineering Data

### The Fan System
- 400mm carbon fiber fan, rear-mounted
- 8.5 kW electric motor, 48V system, spins to 7,000 RPM
- Inspired by 1978 Brabham BT46B "fan car" (Murray's own F1 design)
- Solves diffuser stall at low speeds via boundary layer control
- Creates Venturi effect underneath the car
- Eliminates need for traditional wings, splitters, or aggressive aero bodywork
- Six driver-selectable modes: Auto, Braking, High Downforce, Streamline, V-Max Boost, Test
- Braking Mode: deploys rear aerofoils to 45°, maxes fan speed, doubles downforce, cuts stopping from 150 mph by ~10 meters
- High Downforce: channels air through underbody ducts, +50% total downforce
- Streamline: closes floor vents, fan fills trailing wake = "virtual long tail", -12.5% drag
- V-Max Boost: ram-air induction + fan = ~50 additional PS, ~690 hp total
- At 155 mph: ~220 kg downforce. At top speed: ~460 kg downforce
- No rear wing, no front splitter needed

### Brabham BT46B History
- 1978 Swedish Grand Prix, Niki Lauda driving
- Fan positioned as "engine cooling" device — loophole
- Created massive ground effect suction
- Won its only race, then voluntarily withdrawn by Bernie Ecclestone
- Other teams protested; Ecclestone pulled it to preserve FOCA unity
- Murray's original concept, now perfected 45+ years later for road use

### Cosworth GMA V12 Engine
- 3,994 cc (4.0L), naturally aspirated, 65° V angle
- 663 PS (654 hp) at 11,500 RPM
- 467 Nm (344 lb-ft) at 9,000 RPM
- Redline: 12,100 RPM — highest-revving production road car engine ever
- Reaches redline in 0.3 seconds (28,400 revs per second)
- Weight: 178 kg (392 lb) — lightest supercar V12 ever
- Power density: 166 PS/liter — highest of any NA road car engine
- Compression ratio: 14:1 (road version)
- Bore: 81.5mm, Stroke: 63.8mm (oversquare for high-RPM operation)
- 71% of peak torque available at 2,500 RPM

### Materials
- Block & heads: high-strength aluminum alloy
- Crankshaft: steel, 13 kg (28.7 lb) — lightest V12 crankshaft
- Connecting rods: titanium
- Valves: titanium (key to 12,100 RPM redline — lighter than steel, reduces valve float)
- Clutch housing: titanium
- Pistons: metal matrix composite (MMC)
- Exhaust: inconel and titanium
- Gold heat shielding in engine bay (reflecting IR to protect carbon fiber)

### Other Key Specs
- 4 throttle bodies (vs 12 in McLaren F1's BMW V12)
- DOHC, 4 valves per cylinder, variable valve timing
- Direct fuel injection
- Dry sump lubrication
- 12 individual ignition coils
- Xtrac 6-speed H-pattern manual transmission

### Vehicle
- Dry weight: 997 kg (2,198 lb) — sub-1-tonne
- Power-to-weight: 501 kW/tonne
- Central driving position, 3-seat layout (driver center, passengers flanking)
- Carbon fiber monocoque chassis
- 0-60 mph: ~2.9 seconds
- Top speed: 226 mph (364 km/h)
- Price: £2.36 million, 100 units, all sold in 48 hours
- T.50s Niki Lauda track variant: 25 units, 772 PS, 852 kg, up to 1,500 kg downforce

### Aero Development
- Racing Point F1 team (now Aston Martin) wind tunnel used for development
- 40% scale model testing
- Active rear spoilers work in conjunction with fan

### Murray Quotes/Stories
- Set brief for "greatest naturally aspirated road car engine ever"
- Originally wanted two driving modes (high power vs low torque); Cosworth dismissed it, Murray later apologized as the engine was perfect
- Cosworth exceeded power, weight, and power density targets
- Murray listed all McLaren F1 deficiencies he wanted to fix
