### Engineering Depth: 9.1/10
The central thesis, that the flyback is a complication for people who time things rather than a complication for people who collect them, is argued at mechanism level: reset-hammer wear analysis, the three-press dead-time gap, column-wheel sequencing vs cam-actuated mush, vertical-clutch no-jump restart under repeated flyback cycles. The 80h-at-4Hz barrel budget is treated as a torque-budget question (chronograph draw vs barrel tail amplitude) rather than a brochure figure. Forged carbon section goes beyond press-release material: chopped-fiber compression molding, isotropic behavior for cushion cases, honest thickness accounting (carbon bought weight and blackness, not slimness), and the original's black-chrome coating as a wear-at-the-edges contrast. The caseback-insert question is the article's strongest original engineering contribution: threads in chopped-fiber resin are unreliable, metal inserts are the industry answer, and TAG Heuer has not said which. The dual-scale flange is read as two instruments on one ring with correct pulsometer/tachymeter operation. Minor deduction: pusher-force / flyback actuation load figures are unpublished, and the article correctly flags rather than invents them.
