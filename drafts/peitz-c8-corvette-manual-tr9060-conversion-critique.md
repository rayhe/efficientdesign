## 5. Automotive Depth — 90/100

The integration-over-invention thesis is the article's strongest technical thread and is correct: Tremec's envelope-sharing decision is what makes this a bolt-in, and Peitz's work is in the systems around the gearbox. The MoTeC M182/M150 split is explained accurately, including the dual role of the M150 (DCT control or ignition-cut manual control) and the standalone DCT controller's implications for kit cars and restomods. The no-park-lock observation is traced to a real design assumption in the factory car rather than presented as a quirk.

Minor deduction: the article does not address gear ratios at all. A six-speed manual replacing an eight-speed DCT changes the ratio spread and the cruising-rpm picture, and readers weighing the swap will want to know whether first gear is shorter and whether sixth is a usable overdrive. That data is not yet published by Tremec for the TR-9060, which is worth stating rather than leaving the gap silent.

**Score: 90/100**
