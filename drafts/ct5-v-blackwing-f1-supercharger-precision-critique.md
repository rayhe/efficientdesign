# Critique: CT5-V Blackwing F1 Collector Series — Supercharger Precision
## Article #88 | Writer: Elena Voss | Category: Cars

---

### Critic 1: Technical Accuracy (Automotive Engineering)
**Score: 9.2/10**

Strengths:
- Supercharger drive ratio figures (3.14 → 3.24) confirmed via GM Authority's exclusive technical breakdown
- Eaton TVS R1740 designation correct for the CT5-V Blackwing's 1.74L blower
- "Twin four-lobe rotors" accurately describes the TVS twin-rotor architecture
- Precision Package specs precisely match Car & Driver/Motor Authority reporting: 70% front springs, 1,000% rear cradle bushing, 2.8° front camber, 2.0° rear camber, 11% stiffer front sway bar
- $18,000 package price confirmed across multiple sources
- Carbon-ceramic brake thermal stability claims are technically sound
- Magnetorheological fluid description (iron particles 3-10 μm) is consistent with published MR fluid specifications
- The 3.2% rotor speed increase math checks out (3.24/3.14 ≈ 1.032)
- Correct identification that this is a Roots-type supercharger

Minor issues:
- Article states rear springs "60% stiffer" — Motor Authority's detailed review specifies 57%. Autoblog rounds to 60%. Acceptable but the more precise figure exists.
- "0.4 degrees more than the standard car allows" for front camber: Motor Authority says "up from 2.5" (0.3 more). Autoblog says "0.4 more than stock." Different baseline definitions (track alignment vs max stock). Acceptable given source disagreement.
- The thermodynamics explanation about plenum volume as "thermal reservoir" is an interpretation — GM only said "helps to better manage charge-air temperatures." The mechanism described is plausible but not officially detailed.
