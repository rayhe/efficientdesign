# Critique: H. Moser Endeavour Flyback AgenClutch Chronograph
## Article #84 | Draft 1 | Marcus Thorne

### 1. Technical Accuracy — 9.0/10
The article correctly describes the AgenClutch as a patented friction-based horizontal coupling, distinguishing it from both conventional horizontal (toothed) and vertical (coaxial disc) clutches. The HMC 730's derivation from the HMC 902 through removal of the automatic winding system is accurately presented per Hodinkee, Monochrome Watches, and the H. Moser official spec sheet. Specifications (42mm, 13.2mm, 383 components, 49 jewels, 21,600 vph, 72-hour power reserve, ref. 1730-1200) are verifiable against multiple sources. The description of the double tulip spring shock-absorption mechanism aligns with Time and Tide's 2022 technical reporting. The snail cam retrograde minute mechanism is correctly attributed. Minor note: the claim that vertical clutches add "1 to 2 mm" is a reasonable generalization but not sourced to a specific measurement; the principle is sound even if the exact figure is approximate. Heuer's 1887 oscillating pinion patent date is correct.

The comparison of the AgenClutch's geometric advantage (horizontal plane = no height penalty) against the Rolex 4130's vertical stack is an original analytical contribution. The snail cam discussion connecting subdial legibility problems to central-hand ambiguity is well-constructed.
