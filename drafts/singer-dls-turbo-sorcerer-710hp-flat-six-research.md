# Research: Singer DLS Turbo "Sorcerer" — EfficientDesign #259 (cars)

## Topic
Singer Vehicle Design DLS Turbo — the turbocharged follow-up to the Dynamics and Lightweighting Study (DLS), first customer car "Sorcerer" presented January 2026. Engineering angle: a 3.8L twin-turbo flat-six that revs to 9,000 rpm by cooling itself halfway — water-cooled four-valve heads on air-cooled cylinders, the same hybrid-cooling trick Porsche used on the 959.

## Sourced facts
- Announced June 2023; run up the hill at Goodwood Festival of Speed weeks later. First customer car presented January 2026, three years after announcement. (Negoni Labs, Nov 2026; The Drive, Jan 25, 2026; paultan.org, Jan 23, 2026)
- First car named "Sorcerer" by its owner; track-focused configuration; graduated Fantasia Blue darkening toward the tail; Champagne-finished double-seven-spoke magnesium centre-lock wheels. (Singer press release via THE SHOP, Jan 2026; paultan.org)
- Engine: 3.8-litre twin-turbo flat-six, four valves per cylinder, 710 hp SAE net, 750 Nm (553 lb-ft), 9,000 rpm redline. 2023 announcement quoted "over 700 hp"; Hagerty's 2023 piece quoted 690 bhp — the 710 hp SAE net figure is the January 2026 Sorcerer specification. (Singer via THE SHOP; Negoni Labs; The Drive)
- Cooling: water-cooled four-valve cylinder heads combined with air-cooled cylinders and an electrically powered horizontal fan. First Singer engine with this layout. Same hybrid approach Porsche used on the 959. (Singer via THE SHOP; The Drive; Hot Hardware)
- Turbos: twin variable-geometry turbochargers, electric wastegates, air-to-water intercooling. (Singer 2023 announcement via carcody; Negoni Labs)
- Gearbox: new six-speed manual developed with Ricardo, rear-wheel drive only. (Negoni Labs; The Drive)
- Exhaust: titanium and Inconel, side exits. (Negoni Labs; AutoTrader NZ)
- Body: all-new carbon-fibre bodywork, CFD-designed, NACA ducts and added intakes for cooling and turbo breathing. Two aero states: road (ducktail spoiler, lower-drag front fascia) and track (adjustable high-downforce rear wing, deep front splitter). Extra parts delivered in flight cases so the owner can swap. (Hagerty UK, Jun 2023, updated Oct 2026; Negoni Labs)
- Chassis: double-wishbone front suspension; track-focused suspension with bespoke dampers and remote adjustment available. Five-mode traction and stability system developed with Bosch (Road, Sport, Track, Weather, Off). (Negoni Labs; Hagerty UK)
- Wheels/brakes: forged magnesium centre-lock wheels, 19-inch front, 20-inch rear, Michelin Pilot Sport Cup 2 or Cup 2R, 345/30 rear. Carbon-ceramic discs (CCM-R) with monobloc calipers. (Negoni Labs; AutoTrader NZ; Hagerty UK)
- Heritage: tribute to the Porsche 934/5, which won six of eight rounds in the 1977 SCCA Trans-Am championship. Founder Rob Dickinson, age 12, saw a Super 8 film of the 1977 Watkins Glen 6 Hours and remembered the 911 morphing into boxed hips, gaping intakes, and a giant double-planed rear wing. (Hagerty UK)
- DLS origin: naturally aspirated 4.0L flat-six developed with Williams Advanced Engineering and the late Hans Mezger, ~500 hp at 9,000 rpm, 75 cars. DLS Turbo builds on that engine and chassis work. (BIDR Auctions, Aug 2026; The Drive)
- Singer CEO Mazen Fawaz (2023): "the car I have in mind is a Ferrari F40." (Negoni Labs)
- Production: plan understood to be 99 cars; Singer has not confirmed a number. Builds finished at Singer's Oxfordshire, England facility; company based in California. Waiting list ran to end of 2027 at announcement; company employed 500+ people, ~350 in Los Angeles. (Negoni Labs)
- Weight: not published. (Negoni Labs)
- Price: Singer has not announced one. NOT a story angle — no pricing/markup coverage per site rules. (Negoni Labs)

## Corrections and additions (round-1 fact-check, 2026-11-23)
- Timeline: announced June 2023; shown at Goodwood Festival of Speed July 2023 (weeks later, NOT announced at Goodwood). June 2023 to January 2026 is 2 years 7 months, NOT three years.
- Redline is exactly 9,000 rpm. No source supports "past 9,000" or "9,000+".
- NA DLS vs Turbo are different engines: NA DLS is 4.0L (~500 hp, 125 hp/L), Turbo is 3.8L (710 hp, ~187 hp/L). Not "same engine plus turbos".
- NA DLS verified specs: 4.0L, 500 hp, 9,000 rpm, 990 kg curb weight, titanium connecting rods, dual oil circuits, magnesium Hewland six-speed manual, revealed Goodwood 2018, 75 cars. (evo, Jul 2018; stuttcars; carcollectorinternational)
- Ferrari F40 WAS the fastest production car at launch: 201 mph, first production car past 200 mph. Do not write "not the fastest of its era".
- Porsche 911 Turbo S (992.1) for comparison: 640 hp SAE @ 6,750 rpm, 3.745L twin-VTG flat-six, 590 lb-ft, 7,200 rpm max engine speed, 168.4 hp/L, 8-speed PDK, AWD, 3,636 lbs. (Porsche press data)
- Porsche has used variable-geometry turbos on the 911 Turbo since the 997 Turbo (2006), first series petrol VTG. (Porsche Newsroom, verified by fact-check)
- Sorcerer interior: leather and Alcantara seat upholstery with contrast Champagne piping. (paultan.org)
- 934/5: Trans-Am racer, NOT "endurance racer". Won 6 of 8 rounds, 1977 SCCA Trans-Am.
- Singer employs 500+ people (~350 Los Angeles). Do not call it "a small company".
- NOT published by Singer: weight, 0-60 mph, top speed, boost pressure, compression ratio, bore/stroke, fuel/injection type, dry-sump status. Do not invent these.
- Ricardo: describe only as the British transmission specialist that developed the six-speed manual with Singer. Do not assert F1/Le Mans/McLaren work without a citation.

## Corrections and additions, round 2 (2026-11-23)
- Dickinson quote (Hagerty UK): "Since Singer began, I've wanted to return to that moment, collaborate with our clients and celebrate that car, the 934/5." Sourced; the "since Singer began" intent IS in the Hagerty piece.
- NA DLS materials: extensive use of carbon fiber, magnesium, and titanium, 990 kg curb weight. (evo, Jul 2018; stuttcars)
- Exhaust temperatures (general engineering knowledge): petrol exhaust gas ~950-1,000 C at full boost; diesel ~750-850 C. VGT vane failure modes: warping, seizure, creep, actuator/linkage distortion.
- Porsche 959 (general knowledge): 2.85L twin-turbo flat-six, about 450 hp (~158 hp/L), ~7,200 rpm redline. Split cooling precedent.
- Derivable from published Sorcerer figures (computed, not Singer-published): torque density 750/3.8 = ~197 Nm/L; torque at peak power = 710 hp at 9,000 rpm = ~562 Nm; peak BMEP = 4*pi*750/0.0038 = ~24.8 bar.
- Hypothetical power-to-weight (clearly labelled, NOT a published figure): IF weight matched NA DLS 990 kg, 710 hp = ~717 hp/tonne. NA DLS: 500/990 = ~505 hp/tonne. 992.1 Turbo S: 640 hp / 1,649 kg = ~388 hp/tonne.
- 2023 vs 2026 delta: Hagerty (2023) quoted 690 bhp; Singer (Jan 2026) quotes 710 hp SAE net for Sorcerer. Whether a real gain or a different yardstick is unstated; Singer has published no other change between show car and customer car.
- Why 3.8L not 4.0L: Singer has not said. Hypothesis only (label as inference): smaller displacement leaves more metal between bores and more thermal headroom per litre, which matters with air-cooled cylinders under boost. Bore/stroke unpublished.
- Negoni Labs article title ("three years after Goodwood") is quoted verbatim in sources but the interval is wrong (2 years 7 months); annotate it in the article source list.

## Corrections (2026-11-23, round-5 factual critique)
- Porsche 959 redline is about 7,300 rpm, not 7,200 (rev counter red-lined at 7,300; Car and Driver: "just under the 7300 redline"). Line 58 above carries the wrong figure.
- Porsche 911 Turbo S (992.1) power density is about 171 hp/L (640 / 3.745), not 168.4 hp/L. The 168.4 figure on line 45 comes from Porsche's press sheet, which uses a rounded 3.8L denominator. Use 171 hp/L for computed comparisons.
- The split-cooling precedent is not 959-only: Porsche used water-cooled heads on air-cooled cylinders in the Group C 956/962 (Type 935/76, from 1982, 2.65L, 620 hp at 8,200 rpm), and the 959's engine was derived from that race unit. "Porsche tried this once" holds only for production road cars.
- F1 piston speed near 25 m/s is the V8-era figure (18,000 rpm). Current 1.6L turbo-hybrid F1 engines run about 20 m/s. Qualify by era when citing.
- Turbo S BMEP at peak power (derived): 640 hp at 6,750 rpm implies about 675 Nm and about 22.7 bar, against about 18.6 bar for the DLS Turbo at peak power (assuming peak power at 9,000 rpm). Compare peak-power BMEP to peak-power BMEP; the 26.8 bar Turbo S figure is at peak torque.
