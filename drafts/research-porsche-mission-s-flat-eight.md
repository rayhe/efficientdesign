# Research: Porsche Mission S flat-eight halo supercar (#215, cars)

## News peg
- Oct 7, 2026: Porsche Capital Markets Day, Weissach. New CEO Michael Leiters (ex-McLaren Automotive CEO, Ferrari technology chief) unveils "Sportwagenschmiede '35" strategy (sports car forge '35).
- Mid-engined "super sports car platform" confirmed, positioned clearly above the 911. First range-topping halo since the 918 Spyder (production ended 2015).
- Mission S concept previews the platform: debut October 15, 2026, New York (per evo).
- Official language is hedged: "potential" platform. No power figure, cylinder count, gearbox, production date, or price confirmed.
- Sources: Traction (tractionins.com, Oct 2026), Auto Express (Oct 7, 2026), Autocar Pro, WhichCar, autocarindia.

## Engine rumor
- September 2026, Handelsblatt: flagship codenamed S1 will use a twin-turbo flat-eight (eight-cylinder boxer). If accurate, first flat-eight in a production Porsche road car.
- Counter-report: Automotive News says Leiters favors a V8; the flat-plane crank V8 from the Lamborghini Temerario cited as a candidate (VW Group parts sharing is an explicit strategy pillar).
- Le Tribunal du Net: any production version likely paired with electric assistance to match hybrid rivals.
- French outlet note: Porsche has not confirmed pure combustion vs hybrid.

## Porsche flat-eight history (verified)
- Type 753 (1962): 1.5L air-cooled flat-eight for F1, designed by Hans Mezger. Bore x stroke 66.0 x 54.6 mm. 180 hp at 9,200 rpm, 10.0:1 compression. Magnesium crankcase split vertically, one-piece crankshaft in nine main bearings, titanium connecting rods, four camshafts (two per head), two valves per cylinder, individual cylinder barrels with Ferral (spray-on molybdenum/steel) bore coating. 341 lb with exhaust and clutch. Build time 100-220 hours per engine. Debuted Dutch GP May 1962; delivered Porsche's only F1 win as a constructor: Dan Gurney, 1962 French GP at Rouen. Still down on power vs Coventry-Climax and BRM V8s. Air-cooled in an F1 car.
- Type 771 (1962-68): 2.0L flat-eight, 240 hp. Used in 718, 904 (6 of 16 factory cars), 906, 910, 907, 909 Bergspyder.
- 914/8: two experimental road cars with detuned flat-eight; one was Ferry Porsche's 60th birthday gift.
- Recent prototype: 5.0L twin-turbo flat-eight, ~750 hp / 738 lb-ft, 9,000 rpm, tested in Cayman-based mule with RWD and manual. Never reached production. A former VW Group powertrain chief said a 5.0L flat-eight was probably too big for the 911, one reason it suits a mid-engined car.
- Ruf: twin-turbo flat-eight at 2026 Goodwood FoS, 1,000+ hp / 1,000+ Nm (covered separately in ruf-b8-erprober-flat-eight article; differentiate: Ruf is a tuner prototype, this is Porsche's factory halo platform).

## Strategy context (Sportwagenschmiede '35)
- Fewer, more expensive cars: break-even below 200,000 units/yr (vs 279,449 delivered 2025); average top-model price up ~20%; ~20% fewer variants; workforce -25%, management -40%.
- Powertrain mix: combustion, PHEV, EV side by side; roughly two combustion/hybrid models per EV.
- 718 Boxster/Cayman: electric only, debut 2027, first full production year 2028. Confirms the mid-engine EV at entry, combustion flagship at top, 911 in the middle. Leiters: 911 will never be electric.
- At least one brand-defining new product per year through 2030.

## Engineering angle (original contribution)
- Why a flat-eight suits a mid-engine halo: perfect primary and secondary balance (piston pairs cancel in opposition, no balance shafts), low center of gravity, short crankshaft length (boxer is wide but short, good behind the cabin where width exists), low polar moment.
- Why not a V8: Porsche's engineering identity is the boxer; a flat-eight keeps the flat-six's low CoG logic while adding displacement. Against the Temerario V8 rumor: the flat-plane V8 has balance compromises a boxer-8 does not need to solve.
- Packaging: the boxer's width is the historical objection for the 911 (rear overhang), which is why a dedicated mid-engine platform is the first architecture that can actually take it.
- Turbocharging a flat-eight: short exhaust runners to turbos mounted low and wide, heat management in a wide engine bay; Porsche's eTurbo know-how from the 992.2 Turbo S (twin electric turbochargers, 145,000 rpm) is directly transferable.
- Open questions: confirmed displacement, hybrid or not, gearbox (PDK certain; manual unlikely at this tier), weight target, production date. All rumor until Oct 15 at the earliest.
