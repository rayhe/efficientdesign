# Research — Casio G-Shock MRG-D5000 Blue Sapphire (Blue Rose Project) (#208)

Topic: the engineering of the MRG-D5000-BDR, Casio's $80,000 blue-sapphire-cased G-Shock announced October 1, 2026 — screwless multi-component sapphire case/bezel joined with kigumi-style interlocks around a DLC Grade 5 titanium core, 20 pieces worldwide. Watches category, byline Marcus Thorne.

## Core facts

- Announced October 1, 2026 (Casio press release via PRNewswire / HinduBusinessLine). Model: MRG-D5000-BDR "Blue Sapphire." Priced at $80,000 USD (£67,000 GBP / €80,000). Limited to 20 pieces worldwide; each hand-assembled by a single master technician at Casio's Yamagata factory; ships in a full glass presentation box.
- Development codename: **Blue Rose Project**, a nod to the flower nature never figured out how to grow on its own. MR-G line launched 1996, so this is the line's 30th anniversary piece. Not named Dream Project #3 (creator Kikuo Ibe has retired), but "DREAM PROJECT" is engraved on the caseback as homage and the "D" in MRG-D5000 pays tribute. (Hodinkee)
- Case and bezel rendered entirely in synthetic blue sapphire crystal. Multi-component construction assembled with **no screws at all**. Interlocking assembly framework inspired by **kigumi**, the Japanese timber-joinery tradition of joining wood without nails, screws, or glue. (Casio release; Hypebeast; HiConsumption)
- Why screwless: sapphire is Mohs 9, one notch below diamond, nearly scratchproof. Hardness and toughness are opposites. Every screw hole is a stress concentrator where cracks start in a brittle material, so the sapphire components interlock like a three-dimensional puzzle to absorb and disperse impact forces. (watchdavid; europesays)
- Inner structure: the sapphire exterior surrounds a black **DLC-coated Grade 5 titanium** inner case. Casio analyzed the force transmitted to the sapphire during impact and developed a shock-resistant structure optimized to protect the material rather than merely house it. (Casio release; Fratello)
- Sapphire supply: blue sapphire starts as ingots grown over several months; Casio selects sections with the desired color concentration and transparency. The material is very difficult to machine into small components, so the R&D team shaped the parts to resist chipping and removed screw holes and other features that could cause cracking. (Fratello)
- Dimensions: 51.3 x 43.2 x 16.45mm (16.5mm). The thickness accommodates the material's structural requirements. (Hypebeast; Stuff.tv hands-on: "almost a cuboid," but comfortable on the wrist thanks to soft Dura Soft rubber strap.)
- Dial: stainless steel brick pattern referencing the original 1983 DW-5000 brick face, finished with blue PVD coating. MR-G mark, "Shock Resist," and other lettering rendered in 24K gold. Blue vapor-deposition brick-pattern face with 24-carat gold lettering. (Fratello; Hypebeast)
- Caseback: screw-lock, Grade 5 titanium with DLC coating, set with a blue sapphire insert, engraved with DREAM PROJECT and the individual serial number (1/20 to 20/20).
- Module 3584 with **Gallium Tough Solar** (Casio: outstanding durability under intense light, highly efficient charging) and Multi-Band 6 radio-controlled timekeeping. STN LCD (negative display); world time (five zones selectable from 38 cities); stopwatch; countdown timer; five daily alarms; full-auto LED backlight. 20 bar / 200m water resistance. (Fratello; Stuff.tv)
- Strap: Dura Soft rubber with the same brick pattern, raised details at different angles to play with light; new titanium clasp with a release button designed for refined tactile response. (Fratello)

## History: two predecessors and a failed prototype

- 2017: at G-Shock's 35th anniversary event in New York, Ibe unveiled DW5600 prototypes with case, bracelet, and all rendered in sapphire crystal. They could not survive Casio's own shock testing, so the concept spent nearly a decade in limbo. (Hodinkee)
- Dream Project #1 (2018, 35th anniversary): G-D5000-9JR in solid 18k yellow gold, 35 pieces, ¥7.7 million (~$70,000 USD). Case, bezel, bracelet, and even screws in 18k gold. Sold via ballot in May 2019, deliveries from January 2020. Ibe: "It took five years for our dream project to achieve an ideal impact-resistant structure for gold, which is delicate yet 1.5 times heavier than stainless steel." (SJX; Hypebeast; The Value)
- Dream Project #2 (2023, 40th anniversary): G-D001, a piece unique shaped with generative-AI design assistance, full-metal shock-resistant structure without resin buffering. Auctioned at Phillips for charity, hammered at $400,050. (The Value)
- The MRG-D5000 is the most expensive production G-Shock ever, dethroning the gold Dream Project on both price and rarity. Six units reach the US on a staggered schedule between December 2026 and June 2027. (HiConsumption)

## Siblings (the trickle-down)

- MRG-B5000SA-2 "Super Blue": 500 pieces, $6,600. Blue AIP-coated COBARION bezel; facet-cut sapphire crystals at the upper and lower case portions; Grade 5 titanium; module 3541. (Revolution; HiConsumption)
- GMW-B5000SB-1A2 "Gem Accents": $860 (¥... ). Stainless steel; four blue sapphire gemstones set at the characteristic bezel-screw positions; module 3539. Both land in November at gshock.com, the SoHo store, and select retailers. (Revolution; HiConsumption)
- The three models demonstrate three levels of sapphire integration: accent stones (GMW), prominent stone features (MRG-B5000SA), full structural exterior (MRG-D5000). (AseanNews)

## Materials-science anchors

- Sapphire = corundum, aluminum oxide (Al2O3), Mohs 9. Blue color comes from trace doping (Fe/Ti intervalence charge transfer); synthetic blue sapphire is lab-grown (Verneuil flame-fusion historically; larger boules for structural parts via Kyropoulos/Czochralski-type growth over months). Textbook fracture toughness for single-crystal sapphire is roughly 2-3 MPa√m, versus ~0.7 for ordinary glass and tens-to-hundreds for ductile metals. Hedge: these are reference values, not Casio's.
- The engineering inversion: in the MRG-D5000, the sapphire is not the load path. The DLC Grade 5 titanium inner case is. The sapphire is armor that must itself be protected from bending moments and point loads; the kigumi interlock distributes impact into bearing contact across large areas instead of concentrating it at fasteners.

## Analysis angles (original contribution)

1. **Hardness is not toughness**: the defining materials-science lesson, stated plainly. A G-Shock exists to survive sharp impacts; sapphire survives scratches. Choosing sapphire for a shock watch picks the worst material for the brand's defining test. The product only makes sense because the engineers redesigned force transmission instead of trying to invent tougher sapphire.
2. **The fastener-free architecture is the product**: screw holes are crack nucleation sites in brittle materials. The kigumi interlock is not decoration or heritage cosplay; it converts concentrated point loads into distributed bearing loads across the sapphire components. That is the mechanism that lets a brittle case pass a shock test it failed in 2017.
3. **The titanium truth**: the sapphire is not structural. The DLC Grade 5 titanium inner case carries the load; the sapphire shell is carried. Casio's own framing, "protecting that material rather than merely housing it," inverts the normal case relationship. The 16.45mm thickness is the material's tax, paid in wearability.
4. **Twenty pieces is a yield statement**: blue sapphire ingots grow over months and only zones with the right color concentration and transparency qualify. Scarcity here is partly boule economics, not just marketing. Hedge: Casio says it selects sections with the desired color and transparency; the yield inference is mine.
5. **Gold vs sapphire, opposite materials problems**: Dream Project #1 fought gold's softness and 1.5x steel density (mass amplifies impact force; structure solved it). Dream Project sapphire fights low mass, extreme stiffness, and brittle fracture (structure solves it again). Same team, mirrored problem.
6. **Honest ledger (skeptic)**: $80,000 buys a quartz module whose timekeeping accuracy any $300 G-Shock matches; the negative STN display is genuinely less legible (Stuff.tv hands-on); 16.45mm is "almost a cuboid"; there is no sapphire bracelet; if a sapphire component chips, Casio has said nothing about repair or replacement. Sapphire-cased luxury watches are not new (Hublot and others, covered here before). The novelty is the shock-resistance claim, not the material. Price coverage stays out; the number is context for the engineering economics.
