## Scores

### Technical Accuracy: 9.0/10
All hard figures trace to launch reporting published Sep 4-5, 2026 (radical-mag Sep 4, carsfera Sep 5, carbuzz Aug 14), three to four days before publication, and the two fresh sources agree on core numbers: supercharged 6.8-liter GM-based V8 by LME with fewer than 20% original components remaining, CNC-machined heads, titanium valves, forged rotating assembly, MoTeC management, 960 hp current output, Samsonas six-speed sequential with straight-cut dog gears in a rear-mounted transaxle, carbon-fiber prop shaft, Wavetrac differential, aluminium chassis and bodywork, double-wishbone suspension with 3-way Nitron dampers, 20-inch forged aluminium wheels, 771 pounds of downforce from wing/splitter/flat floor, FIA cage, Tillett seats, six-point harnesses, ~80% exterior design carryover to the GTR/GT road cars with H-pattern manual and automatic options. The 1,200-horsepower ceiling is correctly hedged: carsfera reports Jensen's "up to 1,200 hp and 1,100+ lb-ft" claim while radical-mag phrases it as ~1,200 PS and nearly 1,500 Nm; the article presents both framings without asserting either as current output. Heritage claims (1966 Interceptor with Chrysler V8 and Touring body, FF as first production car with AWD and ABS, Kjell Qvale joining in 1970, insolvency 1975, winding up 1993, revivals 1998-2005/2007/2010, rights held by Jensen International Automotive since 2015, David Duerden's aeronautical background and Compotec F1 carbon parts, Jeff Qvale as Kjell's son) all match radical-mag. One genuine discrepancy caught and disclosed rather than smoothed over: carsfera credits AP Racing with the brakes while radical-mag names BG Developments; the article names both sources and flags the badge as provisional. The "I have not driven it" disclaimer is explicit, and every unpublished figure (weight, 0-60, top speed, price, lap times) is listed as unpublished rather than estimated.

### Engineering Depth: 9.1/10
The central thesis, that the gearbox matters more than the horsepower figure, is argued at mechanism level: helical versus straight-cut tooth geometry and the axial thrust penalty of helical gears, dog engagement versus synchromesh cones with ignition-cut shift mechanics, rear transaxle mass distribution, carbon prop-shaft torsional stiffness and critical speed, and the Wavetrac's wave-cut side gears maintaining preload under zero axle load. The Roots supercharger section loads the dice correctly: positive-displacement boost tied to crank speed, forged rotating assembly handling the boost, titanium valvetrain buying back reciprocating mass, CNC-ported heads matching cylinder filling to blower delivery. The aluminium-bodywork-from-a-carbon-man argument is the article's strongest original contribution: a small manufacturer choosing hand-formed aluminium over carbon tubs for heritage, repairability, and low-volume sanity, framed as the most contrarian material choice available. Minor deduction: actual shift-time figures and dog-ring count for the Samsonas box are unpublished, and the article correctly flags rather than invents them.

### Originality: 8.9/10
Launch coverage (3-4 days old) led with the 960-hp headline, the Jensen resurrection narrative, and the design. No launch report took the straight-cut/dog-box thesis, the axial-thrust energy argument, the gearbox-as-culture-statement reading, or the carbon-man-chooses-aluminium paradox. The "anyone can make 960 hp; nobody accidentally builds a dog box" framing is a genuine editorial angle. Deduction: the brake-supplier discrepancy, while handled honestly, is flagged rather than resolved, which was the right call but slightly blunts the section's authority.
