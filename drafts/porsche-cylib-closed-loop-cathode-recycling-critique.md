## Critic 1: Technical Accuracy

**Score: 8.5/10**

**Strength:** All announcement facts check out against the primary source (Porsche Newsroom, 17/09/2026): the 100% recycled cathode active material claim, Li/Ni/Co/Mn element list, water-based processing since May 2026, material account tracking, 2028 availability target, Scharnagl and Schwich quotes, cylib founding (2022, Aachen, by Dr. Lilian Schwich, Paul Sabarny, Dr. Gideon Schwich), and the "initial tests indicated technical feasibility / further results H2 2026" hedge are all faithfully represented. The boundary discipline ("cathode active material only, not the whole cell") is the article's most important accuracy decision and it is handled correctly.

**Improvement — Cost claim needs its hedge confirmed:** "cathode active material typically accounts for roughly half the cell's cost" is a defensible industry figure for NMC cells (cathode materials are the dominant cost component), and the draft hedges it with "typically" and "roughly." Acceptable as written, but this is a number Ray would notice; it survives on the double hedge. Verdict: keep, do not strengthen the claim.

**Secondary note:** The spec table says "hydrometallurgy Dormagen" for cylib's sites; the release specifies the Dormagen hydrometallurgical processing is "currently under development." Fix: add "(under development)" to that cell so the table doesn't overstate operational status.

## Critic 2: Originality of Angle

**Score: 9/10**

**Strength:** This does what almost no recycling coverage does: it treats the announcement as a crystal-chemistry problem rather than a tonnage story. "Purity is the milestone, not recovery" reframes the entire genre of battery-recycling headlines, and the material-account-as-bookkeeping-design section is genuinely novel editorial framing — nobody's press-release recap discusses dedicated material accounts vs. mass-balance accounting. The supply-chain-resilience reading ("a sustainability costume") is sharp without being cynical.

**Improvement:** The EU battery-passport paragraph correctly identifies the regulatory tailwind but stays vague on what the passport requires. A one-sentence specification (documented provenance and recycled-content thresholds for batteries placed on the EU market) would ground the claim. Minor; the hedge as written is safe.
