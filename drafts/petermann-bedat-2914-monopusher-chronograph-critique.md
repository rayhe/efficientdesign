Weekdays verified with `date`: Nov 12 Thursday, Nov 13 Friday, Nov 15 Sunday, Nov 16 Monday, Nov 26 Thursday. Math verified: 30/36.5 = 82.2% movement fill; 38.6 − 36.5 = 2.1mm; 13.0 − 11.6 = 1.4mm; 18,000 vph = 5 beats/sec = 0.2s steps.

**Attribution handled correctly:** Valjoux 23 lineage attributed to SJX; free-sprung balance attributed to Rehaut 1775 (brand materials say large balance with Breguet overcoil); "long-lasting operation and energy transfer" attributed to Bersenti's account; 2941 thickness comparison pinned to Monochrome's figures (13mm), with Watchonista's 13.7mm noted as a differing secondary figure and not used. No lume claims anywhere. No production total beyond the 20 platinum pieces. No accuracy figure invented.

**Deductions:** two general-knowledge claims are stated without a source anchor: horizontal clutches causing a seconds-hand jump on starting, and gold wearing faster than brass or steel. Both are hedged ("can make", framed as a trade-off discussion) and standard horological knowledge, but a citation would close them. Minor.

## 2. Engineering Depth — 9.0

Strongest dimension. The jumping-minute-counter-as-metrology passage (decoupled counter, snail cam, jumper spring, torque theft from the going train), the subtraction argument (rattrapante removal shrinking the case, not just the movement), the slow-beat trade (0.2s resolution vs wear and steadiness), the gold-wheel paradox (soft material, display finishing, redesign as the answer), the horizontal-vs-vertical clutch choice, and the movement-fill geometry (82%, soldered lugs because there is no room for anything else) are all original analysis, not press-release summary.

**Deduction:** the article never converts the jumping counter's benefit into a number (what reading error a dragging counter introduces mid-minute at a typical glance). Optional; the qualitative argument carries it.

## 3. Originality — 8.7

The measurement-error framing ("how much of its reading is the thing measured, and how much is the instrument") is the article's own contribution, and the subtraction-as-engineering thesis (keep the complication you use, delete the one you show off) is a genuine editorial position. The honest-ledger comparison against Rexhepi (CHF 150,000 flyback) and Grönefeld (EUR 160,000) reframes the price as a finishing-and-proportion purchase rather than a complication purchase.

**Deduction:** the naming-story paragraph (Tolkien, Patek reversals) is well told but largely retells what Watchonista and Monochrome already published. Acceptable as context, not as analysis.

## 5. Readability / Value — 8.8

A reader who has never heard of Petermann Bédat can follow the whole argument. The spec table is complete and every figure in it appears in the body or a source. Limitations are honest: not handled, no hands-on coverage, no accuracy figure, bracelet unknown, long-term reliability of the jumping counter and gold train unassessable. Seven sources, all linked.

**Deduction:** the monopusher "cannot reset while running" rule is stated as a guard; the article could note in one clause that this is standard monopusher behaviour rather than a 2914-specific feature. Minor.
