# Critique: Three Watches, One Soundboard — AP Supersonnerie Trio
**Article #252 | Elena Voss | November 16, 2026**

## 1. Technical Accuracy (9.0/10)

Launch facts are triple-sourced and internally consistent. Announcement date November 2, 2026, all three references (26591TI.OO.1252TI.08, 26591OR.OO.1252OR.01, 26395BC.OO.D009CR.01), case dimensions (42 x 14mm Royal Oaks, 41 x 13.6mm Code 11.59), and Swiss prices (CHF 351,100 / 391,400 / 325,000) agree across SJX, Dial & Crown, Gear Patrol, Watchonista, and Notebookcheck. Calibre 2953 figures (362 components, 32 jewels, 3 Hz, 72 hours, 30mm x 6mm) are identical in every outlet. The 50-piece titanium limit is confirmed by SJX and WatchGuys. The Code 11.59 reference-number discrepancy is handled correctly: Watchonista prints D002KB.01 while four other outlets print D009CR.01, and the article uses the majority reading and documents the disagreement in the sources block.

Supersonnerie mechanics are described accurately against the technical literature: gongs soldered to a soundboard beneath the movement rather than the mainplate, vented caseback with a cavity, gasket sealing the movement chamber for 20m resistance, redesigned governor with a flexible anchor, shortened quarter-to-minute pause, and the anti-setting security function. The soundboard alloy is handled with proper hedging: Chronos Japan specifies a titanium-copper alloy while SJX and others say titanium, and the article states both rather than picking one silently. RD#1 dating (presented 2015, production 2016) matches Monochrome. The eight-year EPFL programme matches every source.

One residual nit: the article says the Sound Lab work began implicitly with the eight-year programme; Deployant dates the Sound Lab itself to 2006, which would make the programme nine years to RD#1 in 2015. The eight-year figure is what AP and all launch coverage state, so the article is correct to use it, but the 2006 origin is worth a footnote in a future revision. Not a scoring issue.

## 2. Engineering Depth (9.1/10)

The article earns its engineering billing. The guitar analogy is not decoration: it maps strings, bridge, soundboard, body, and sound hole onto gongs, gong stud, soundboard plate, cavity, and caseback vents one by one, and then explains why each mapping matters physically (a parts-loaded mainplate absorbs vibration; a dedicated plate radiates it). The governor section goes beyond the press line to explain why a traditional governor is itself a noise source, which is the kind of second-order thinking the site exists for. The "known quantity" argument for keeping calibre 2953 is a genuine piece of experimental reasoning: same gears, different sound path, so the improvement is attributable.

Could go one step further on the gong metallurgy (why hardened steel, what cathedral gongs would add) and on the acoustic tuning process itself (what "tuned by ear, watch by watch" actually involves), but the sources do not detail either, and inventing them would be worse than omitting them.

## 3. Original Contribution (8.7/10)

Three genuine contributions beyond the source material: (a) the attribution argument for the old movement, framing the reuse of calibre 2953 as a controlled experiment rather than laziness; (b) the wearability contradiction, that the titanium reference best serves the acoustics yet is limited to 50 pieces while the heaviest, most expensive pink gold version is the obtainable one; (c) the honest accounting that repeater volume is measured in silence and the improvement is mostly theoretical in any real environment. The Chopard Full Strike contrast (sapphire gongs vs. soundboard) correctly positions AP's approach against the rival solution and links to the site's own earlier coverage.
