# Research notes: Ming 57.05 Comet (article #184)

## What it is
- Ming 57.05 "Comet", 9th anniversary release, named after the de Havilland Comet (first commercial jet airliner).
- 40mm x 10.33mm, 47.8mm lug-to-lug, 316L stainless steel, 100m WR. Triple-stepped lugs: 9 separate components for lugs alone.
- Patent-pending split-bezel DST worldtimer. Non-DST cities on fixed inner bezel; DST cities on rotating outer bezel. One click = 15 degrees = all DST cities +1 hour against the 24h scale. Spring-loaded ball + recesses lock two positions. Aperture between 8 and 9 o'clock: silver (standard) / red (DST), lumed red at night.
- Movement: Sellita for Ming 330.M2 (SW330 "caller" GMT), 4Hz, 50h PR, 25 jewels, 26.2 x 4.1mm, skeletonized anthracite bridges. GMT hand replaced by rotating 24h disc (blue day / dark shades).
- Comet Tail dial: multilayered blue, overlapping raster grids, animated moire interference pattern that evolves through the day (coupled to the 24h disc).
- Lume: Super-LumiNova X1 — blue emission for daytime hours, orange emission for nighttime hours on the 24h ring; blue hands; red-emission DST indicator. Polar White proprietary lume laser-engraved into the box sapphire crystal (floating 12h markers).
- Strap: cobalt blue goat leather by Jean Rousseau Paris, quick-release spring bars, flying-blade tuck buckle with micro-adjustment. Optional titanium Polymesh bracelet. 20mm lug width.
- CHF 6,950 leather / CHF 7,950 Polymesh, 200 pieces, orders opened Aug 27, 2026.
- Lineage: Ming 19.02 Worldtimer, 29.01 Worldtimer (micro-rotor, titanium). Cottier 1930s worldtime system origin.

## Engineering angles (original contribution)
1. The Cottier blind spot: worldtimers encode fixed offsets; DST changes offsets for a subset of cities twice a year. The standard fix is mental math. Ming's fix moves complexity from the movement to the interface.
2. Why a bezel, not the movement: a 2-state rotation of one sub-group costs a detent ball and two recesses; doing it in the movement (like rare high-end mechanical DST systems) costs parts, thickness, and service headaches. One click, 15 degrees.
3. Moire dial math: two overlapping raster grids, one coupled to the 24h disc (15 deg/hour rotation). Moire fringe amplification means tiny relative rotation produces large visible pattern evolution. The dial encodes time-of-day texturally as well as numerically.
4. Lume as information design: color-coded 24h ring (blue=day, orange=night), red DST status dot. Photoluminescence used as data, not decoration.
5. Honest limitations: (a) I have not handled the watch — all observations from press images and specs; (b) transition-date mismatch: US and EU switch on different dates, so the binary indicator is wrong for one region for ~3 weeks/year (Hodinkee notes Switzerland/US desync); (c) southern-hemisphere DST runs opposite seasons — a binary DST ON can't represent both hemispheres simultaneously; (d) no half/quarter-hour zone handling; (e) 200 pieces likely allocated at ordering opening.

## Fact check notes
- Colors: blue emission = daytime, orange emission = nighttime (Monochrome, Europa Star, HiConsumption agree; Hodinkee body text has a garbled sentence but its specs box agrees: blue day / orange night).
- 24h disc "divided into daytime and nighttime shades of blue" (Hodinkee) — the disc itself is blue/black day-night; the numerals get the color-coded lume.
