## Scores

### Technical Accuracy: 9.0/10
All hard figures trace to July 2026 Goodwood reporting (MotorTrend, Autocar, The Drive, CarBuzz, Autoblog, Motor Illustrated): 4.8L twin-turbo flat-eight, over 1,000 hp, over 1,000 Nm (738 lb-ft), CTR3 mule stretched 100 mm, RUF-developed six-speed manual, Tanner Foust hillclimb runs July 10-12, MOTUL development partner, RUF's "in this form" quote. Porsche 904 concept (5.0L flat-eight, ~750 bhp, 738 lb-ft) attributed to Autocar; "no series-production flat-eight ever" per CarBuzz/The Drive; two 914/8 prototypes per CarBuzz. Arithmetic is the author's own and checks out: 1,000/4.8 = ~208 hp/L, 3.6L-to-1,000-hp = ~278 hp/L, 720/8 = 90-degree firing intervals, 1,000 Nm = 737.6 lb-ft ("just under 738"). The 908 racing claim ("3.0-liter air-cooled flat-eight, more than 8,000 rpm, late 1960s") rests on established history rather than a re-verified primary source in this run, correctly hedged. The "I have not heard this engine" disclaimer is explicit. Deduction: RUF disclosed no bore/stroke/compression/redline/weight, and the article correctly declines to invent them, but that leaves the cooling and crank-damper sections as reasoning rather than reporting, which the article labels.

### Engineering Depth: 9.1/10
The central thesis (displacement as a structural answer to four-figure output, lower per-cylinder peak pressure and knock margin instead of more boost) is argued with numbers, not adjectives. Balance physics section covers mirror-pair primary cancellation, two-section crank phasing for secondary shake, and the 90-degree firing-interval consequences for both gearbox smoothness and turbo spool. The long-crank torsional vibration analysis is the strongest original contribution: it explains the historical absence of the layout, not just its presence. Thermal packaging is framed as an open question rather than a solved one. Deduction: the exhaust-manifold scavenging effect on turbo response is raised and left open; no source publishes the manifold design, so the restraint is correct but the reader is left wanting one more mechanism.

### Originality: 9.0/10
Launch coverage (July 2026) led with the headline figures, the Yellowbird livery, and the "no production flat-eight ever" novelty. No launch report ran the 208-vs-278 hp/L comparison, the per-cylinder work split, the torsional-resonance theory for why the layout stayed shelved, or the firing-interval-to-gearbox coupling argument for the manual. The Porsche-904-concept torque coincidence (738 lb-ft, twice) is noticed but not overplayed. Deduction: the "boost is a tax, displacement is a savings account" framing will recur in the reader's memory of every future forced-induction piece.
