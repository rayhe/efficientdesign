# Critic Panel — Article #160: Three Scales, One Sweep
## Omega Speedmaster Chronoscope Cal. 9908

### 1. Technical Accuracy — 9.0/10
All caliber specifications match published Omega data: Cal. 9908 hand-wound, 32.5×6.4mm, 44 jewels, 4Hz, 60h PR, co-axial escapement, silicon balance spring, twin DLC-coated barrels, METAS Master Chronometer. The distinction between Cal. 9908 (hand-wound, 44 jewels) and Cal. 9900 (auto, 54 jewels, 7.6mm) is correctly stated. The Cal. 321 reissue lateral clutch point is factually correct and addresses a common misconception. Minor quibble: the claim that Cal. 321 was "the last Speedmaster with column wheel + vertical clutch" needs nuance — the original 321 actually used a lateral clutch, and the article correctly states this later, which means the pull-stat's implicit claim that the 321 had both is misleading. The body text fixes this by noting the 321 had column wheel + lateral clutch. 15,000 gauss METAS spec is correct. DLC barrel coating claims are appropriately hedged ("Omega does not disclose the specific DLC composition").

**Deduction:** -1.0 for the pull-stat/intro implying the 321 had both column wheel AND vertical clutch, which the body text then contradicts. The body is right; the intro needs tightening.

### 3. Materials Science — 9.0/10
DLC coating description (vapor-deposited amorphous carbon, ~1/10 coefficient of friction vs steel-on-steel) is accurate. The distinction between DLC as surface treatment vs fluid lubricant is correct. The ta-C speculation (>50 GPa hardness, <20nm roughness) is appropriately hedged as industry standard, not Omega-specific. Bronze Gold alloy description (75% Au, Cu/Pd/Fe/Ga replacing Ag) matches Omega's published metallurgy. The patina mechanism (copper oxidation beneath gold matrix, no verdigris) is correct. DRIE for silicon balance springs is the correct fabrication method.

**Deduction:** -1.0 for not mentioning that Omega's Bronze Gold is also sometimes referred to as Sedna Gold in their other lines (different alloy) — could cause confusion for readers who know Omega's other proprietary golds.

### 4. Horological Depth — 9.5/10
Excellent movement analysis. The column wheel description (toothed pillar, positive mechanical stops, castle turret analogy) is vivid and accurate. Vertical clutch explanation (coaxial friction discs vs lateral gear engagement) correctly identifies the core engineering difference. The comparison between Cal. 9908, 3861, and 321 demonstrates genuine understanding of the Speedmaster movement lineage. The co-axial escapement description (Daniels 1974, two-level escape wheel, reduced sliding contact) is technically sound. The "three analog computers" framing is an original contribution that elevates the piece above standard product coverage.

**Highlight:** The tachymeter/telemeter/pulsometer math breakdowns (3600/t, 0.343×t, 900/t) are genuinely useful engineering content, not decorative.
