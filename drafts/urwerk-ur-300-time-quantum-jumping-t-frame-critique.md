## 1. Technical Accuracy (8.8/10)

The three simultaneous events (72-degree frame jump, bar swap with each carrier pivoting on its own centre, all four prisms rotating one face) are corroborated by Revolution and The 1916 Company, whose presentational sequencing differs but agree the events fire together. The 108-degree derivation (180 minus 72) is taken directly from The 1916 Company's breakdown. The numbering math (prisms carrying hours four apart; three faces, four turns, fourth turn advances one) is quoted from Revolution's walkthrough and the arithmetic checks: 1 → 5 → 9 → 1 → 5 across four hourly jumps.

Rack-and-cam jump description (rack C, cam E, follower D, spring F, 72-degree snap, crown-wheel conversion to axial rotation) follows The 1916 Company's technical breakdown. Materials list (beryllium copper, brass, anodized aluminum, ARCAP AP1D, titanium, LIGA nickel) is per the same source; ARCAP-as-nickel-silver and the LIGA process description are independently correct. Dual Flow Turbine function (air-resistance rotor governing, shock absorption, torque metering) is corroborated by both outlets. AlTiN-is-PVD chemistry is correct, which justifies preferring PE-CVD for the black finish. Beryllium copper's non-magnetic behaviour and valve-seat use are correct.

Two fixes applied during critique: the minute-wheel drive is now hedged as "presumably" per the source's own qualification, and "lug to lug" was corrected to "case length" since neither source specifies lug-to-lug. Minor residual: "a jumping-hours pocket watch from the 1920s" is rhetorical scene-setting rather than a cited claim; acceptable in context but flagged.
