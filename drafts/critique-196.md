## Critic Scores

### 1. Engineering Depth — 9/10
The article earns its EfficientDesign placement: the sintering explanation (green part, ~1,400 C, ~30% linear shrinkage, diamond-only post-firing finishing) is mechanically precise and grounds the whole thesis. The hardness (1,200-1,500 Vickers vs ~200 for 316L) and density (~6 vs 7.9 g/cm3) numbers are real material-science values, not hand-waving. The integrated-bracelet tolerance-stacking argument is the sharpest original contribution: each link is its own mold-and-fire cycle, and the finishing pattern (satin/polish alternation) must be ground in after sintering. The 8.90mm packaging argument with the 72h automatic is a legitimate miniaturization read. One level deeper would be the binder-burnoff debinding step and green-body yield rates, but for editorial scope this is strong.

### 2. Accuracy — 8.5/10
Core claims verified against multiple sources:
- **Unveiled Geneva Watch Days 2026, embargo Sept 2:** Confirmed by WATCHESPEDIA and WorldTempus.
- **First ceramic in the Admiral's history:** Confirmed by WatchTime and the Corum press release via Instore Magazine.
- **39mm x 8.90mm, 50m WR:** Confirmed by WatchTime.
- **Three references, G5 titanium / 5N gold / anthracite skeleton:** Confirmed by WatchTime, press release.
- **Prices 18,900 / 24,900 / 33,000 CHF:** Confirmed by WATCHESPEDIA and Southern Jewelry News (independent agreement).
- **CO231 (172 parts, 29 jewels) / CO232 (181 parts, 28 jewels), 28,800 vph, 72h:** Confirmed by WATCHESPEDIA.
- **Integrated bracelet first for Admiral, five-link satin/polished:** Confirmed by WatchTime.
- **April 2026 redesign, pennants to trapezoidal indices:** Confirmed by WatchTime and press release.
- **Mehmedovic quote:** Verbatim from the Instore Magazine press release transcription.
- **Admiral born 1960, signature form 1983:** Confirmed by press release.

One soft spot: the ~30% shrinkage figure and the 1,400 C sintering temperature are stated as characteristic zirconia values rather than Corum's specific process parameters, which Corum does not publish. They are the right order of magnitude and standard for yttria-stabilized zirconia in watchmaking (Rado, IWC, Omega all cite ~30%), but the article could flag them as industry-typical rather than Corum-specific. The bracelet-link mold geometry ("curved wrist radius", "each subject to that same ~30% journey") is engineering inference, plausible but not Corum-stated. Both are clearly presented as process explanation, not claimed quotes, so this stays at 8.5.

### 3. Writing Quality — 9/10
Clean hook (machine steel wrong vs machine ceramic wrong), tight rhythm, no filler. "A material that laughs at carbide tooling" and "predicted, molded, fired, and only then finished" are sharp. The sea metaphor in the close is earned by the article's own maritime argument, not pasted on. No sycophantic modifiers, no banned phrases, no em-dash crutches. One paragraph ("Multiply that difficulty...") was trimmed during drafting to remove a duplicate sentence; the surviving text reads smoothly.

### 4. Structure — 9/10
Eight sections, each with a job: hook intro, announcement facts, material science, bracelet (the real story), movement specs, heritage, spec table, closing callback. The bracelet section placed before the movements section is the right call, it is the article's thesis. The spec table and sources section follow the site convention. No filler sections.

### 5. Sources — 8.5/10
Five sources: WatchTime (trade press, Geneva Watch Days original), WATCHESPEDIA (spec verification), Instore Magazine (full press release, CEO quote), Southern Jewelry News (price/movement cross-check), WorldTempus (movement reserve, skeleton detail). Two independent confirmations on price and dimensions. Would benefit from one primary Corum technical document or patent, but the press release text serves as the primary. For blog engineering journalism the base is solid.

### 6. Originality — 8.5/10
The launch-day coverage everywhere else is "Corum goes ceramic, here are the colors." The one-shot manufacturing thesis, the tolerance-stacking-through-the-bracelet argument, and the "unforgiving is a manufacturing description, not a marketing word" framing are all fresh angles vs. the standard coverage. The maritime-correctness argument for ceramic (salt/UV/deck furniture) is a genuinely original read on the Admiral brief. Not breaking new ground in materials science, but the synthesis is the article's own.

### 7. Reader Value — 9/10
An engineer reads this and learns: why zirconia shrinks ~30% and cannot be reworked, how hardness and density numbers map to wearability, why a lugless ceramic bracelet is a manufacturing step-change, and how 8.90mm vs 72h reserve is a packaging achievement. A watch enthusiast reads this and understands why the launch is more than a colorway drop, and why ceramic suits a sailing watch. Multiple audience levels, no condescension.
