## Critic Scores

### 1. Engineering Depth — 9/10
The article explains shim-stack mechanics (deflection under pressure, dimensional tolerance chains, wear particulate), spool-valve hydraulics (circumferential ports, spring-loaded spools, pressure-flow relationships), EDM machining (sub-micron precision, no tool wear/deflection), thermal fade physics (viscosity drop → force loss), cavitation mechanics (fluid voids, gas pressure relationship), position-sensitive three-valve architecture, and TASV active motor integration (ball screw, anti-roll bar elimination). Real numbers throughout: ±3% variance, 4% vs 16% fade, 30–120°C range, 15,000 simulations, 35% chassis load reduction, 50% occupant movement reduction. Could go deeper on the actual hydraulic governing equations or specific port profile geometries, but for an editorial audience this is thorough engineering writing.

### 2. Accuracy — 8.5/10
Core claims verified against primary and secondary sources:
- **±3% force variance:** Confirmed by Professional Motorsport World (PMW Magazine) — "tolerance claimed to be within a maximum of +/- 3% of total damping force from valve to valve."
- **4% vs 16% thermal fade (30–120°C):** Confirmed by Multimatic's own technical document (Scribd) — "less than 4% over a typical range of velocities as compared to up to 16% for a shim damper, or up to 14% for a poppet valve."
- **15,000 ZR2 simulations, 35% load / 50% movement reduction:** Confirmed by Autoblog's ZR2 deep-dive.
- **TASV Purosangue debut (2022), anti-roll bar elimination:** Confirmed by Multimatic press release and Motor Authority.
- **Michael Guttilla quote:** Verified against Motor Authority — matches except the article silently drops "For example" from mid-quote without ellipsis. Minor but worth noting for editorial precision.
- **2014 Z/28 production debut, 2016 Le Mans class win, 2017 ZR2 launch:** All confirmed.
- **Larry Holt CTO quote and Dynamic Suspensions 1996 acquisition:** Consistent with Multimatic corporate history.

One flag: the phrase "medical-grade Electrical Discharge Machining" is the article's characterization rather than Multimatic's own language. Multimatic's documents describe EDM capability but don't use the "medical-grade" modifier. The comparison to coronary stents is reasonable (EDM is indeed used for stents), but the compound modifier could imply Multimatic explicitly describes their EDM this way.

But for blog-level engineering journalism, the source base is solid and appropriately primary-weighted.

### 6. Originality — 8.5/10

The article's thesis (shim-stack tuning was art; DSSV made it arithmetic) is a fresh angle vs. the typical "cool tech on cool car" coverage. The ZR2 section avoids the usual truck-bro framing and focuses on the engineering problem (position-sensitive damping). The TASV section explains why anti-roll bar elimination matters from first principles rather than just stating it. Not breaking new ground in damper engineering, but the synthesis and framing are original enough to justify the article.

### 7. Reader Value — 9/10

A car enthusiast reads this and understands why a Z/28 laps faster than a Z51 despite 100+ fewer horsepower, and why a midsize pickup can ride like a desert racer. Multiple audience levels served without condescending to either.
